# Pair-top module and normal certificates

These certificates supply the finite module and character inputs for actions
on twelve or sixteen pairs. Their degrees are the degrees of the **top
actions**. The associated original permutation actions have width 24 or 32;
the top degree never replaces that original width or its normalizer weight.

The data are `../data/pair_top_actions.json`,
`../data/pair_top_modules.jsonl.gz`, `../data/pair_top_groups.jsonl.gz`,
and `../data/pair_top_constructions.json`.
The alphabet has `schema_version: 1`. Each compressed file contains one JSON
object per alphabet action, in exactly the same order. No quotient cache,
abstract group identifier, or same-dimensional module identifier substitutes
for its actual generators or maps.

## Literal domains

| Family | Number | Top degree | Original width | Finite interface |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `twelve` | 301 | 12 | 24 | Complete degree-12 section profiles and top normals |
| `paired8` | 24 | 8 | 32 | Nonbinary outer actions for eight quartet blocks |
| `zero16` | 29 | 16 | 32 | Four primitive four-point blocks with zero ternary kernel |
| `two_affine16` | 15 | 16 | 32 | Two primitive affine eight-point blocks |
| `primitive16` | 20 | 16 | 32 | Affine primitive sixteen-point actions |
| `paired6` | 2 | 6 | 24 | The two outer actions of orders 36 and 72 |

An alphabet action has `id`, `family`, `index`, `degree`, `original_width`,
`order`, `generators`, and `kappa`. The twelve-point actions also have
`branch`: `joint`, `core`, `ternary`, or `paired`. An index is a locator
within its stated family. It is not itself a group-theoretic certificate.

Every permutation row lists the images of points 1 through `degree`.
Generator order is retained. Empty subgroup generator lists mean the
trivial group. The two natural sixteen-point tops are constructed separately
from literal cycles and checked to equal the alternating and symmetric
groups; their universal module argument is not replaced by a finite
submodule enumeration.

## Complete binary modules

A module line contains:

- `id`, `degree`, and the same ordered `generators`;
- `numerators`: the entire invariant-subspace lattice, in increasing
  dimension and then lexicographic order;
- `simple_intervals`: triples `[upper, lower, type]` for every maximal
  proper interval in that lattice;
- `simple_matrices`: a representative of each actual simple module type,
  on the original ordered generators;
- `hom_dimensions`: one row per simple type, one column per numerator;
- `trivial_heads`, and `schur_profiles` with entries
  `[dimension, endomorphism_field_degree, Schur_dimension, maximum_Hom_dimension]`;
- `section_cap`, `cyclic_count`, `product_triples`, and a literal
  `composition_chain`, `composition_multiplicities`, `composition_cap`.

Indices in module records are **zero-based**. A binary vector is an integer
whose bit `i` is the coefficient of point `i+1`. A subspace basis is reduced
over the binary field with ascending pivot positions. A matrix is a list
of such bit-packed images of basis vectors; matrices for different groups
are never compared after forgetting their generator maps.

The verifier visits every binary vector, spans every generator orbit, and
closes the resulting cyclic modules under sums. Every invariant subspace
is a sum of the cyclic modules generated by its vectors. This proves
completeness of the lattice. Its maximal proper intervals are simple.
Two simple intervals have the same type only when the same-generator Hom
equations have a nonzero solution. Thus equally dimensional inequivalent
types remain separate. Those same linear equations compute every Hom and
End dimension.

For a simple type of binary dimension `delta`, if a numerator has a
homogeneous quotient with multiplicity `m`, then
`m * dim End <= dim Hom`. Its Schur demand is therefore at most
`ceil(dim Hom / delta)`. Taking this bound over every numerator proves
`section_cap` for every homogeneous section, including nonsplit
numerators. The independently retained composition chain also bounds the
composition-factor demand used by the primitive and two-block arguments.

For the 24 eight-point actions the replay additionally checks the complete
common-product denominator inequality. For every pair of numerators
`K,W` and every invariant `L` containing their coordinatewise product span,

\[
\dim K/[K,T]+\dim W/[W,T]+\dim L/([L,T]+KW)\le4.
\]

This is 4,479 actual triples. It supplies a finite input to the universal
nonabelian quartet-kernel lemma; the replay does not itself enumerate all
nonabelian pair lifts.

## Every literal top normal

A group line contains `id`, the same `generators`, `structure`,
`characters`, and `normals`. A character entry has a positive integer
`degree` and literal `kernel_generators`. It represents an actual
irreducible character with that kernel and degree. There is one entry per
distinct irreducible kernel, using the least available degree. GAP
recomputes the irreducibles of the supplied permutation group and checks
both fields; a character-list position is not trusted as an identifier.

A normal entry has `generators`, exact `order`, and a `word` of character
indices. Character indices are **one-based**. The kernel intersection of
that exact word must equal that exact normal subgroup. The empty word
has intersection the whole group, so it certifies the trivial quotient.
Repeated isomorphic quotients retain separate normals and words.

The verifier closes the actual irreducible kernels under intersections,
starting at the whole group, and requires equality with the supplied
distinct normal list. This proves all-normal coverage: the faithful
regular representation of any quotient decomposes into irreducibles,
whose inflated kernels intersect in its original kernel. The replay uses
neither `NormalSubgroups` nor a group catalogue. Exact irreducible-character
computation remains a trusted GAP algorithm, not a formally checked
character table proof.

The retained structure data include four actual triples and their binary
averaging map in the eight ternary cases; six actual pairs, all generator
images, the exact pair kernel, and `top_conjugator` transporting that image
to the literal six-point module action in the two paired cases; and a literal
fixed-point-free binary-core element for each of the 29 zero-ternary tops.
Both replays check the same action and original generator order. The
interface checker verifies averaging equivariance, its rank-four image and
dimension-eight kernel, and compatibility of each displayed pair map.

## Scope and remaining mathematical inputs

The constructive coverage replay supplies the bounded computations behind
the three imprimitive domains. For two primitive four-point blocks it
constructs every local normal quotient, every outer automorphism graph and
every order-two element outside the base in the full graph-normalizer
quotient. For four pairs it proves the complete invariant-kernel list by
cyclic closure and tries every pair of lifts of the original generators.
Their union gives 17 imprimitive eight-point actions; adjoining the seven
licensed primitive actions gives the 24-action domain.

For two affine eight-point blocks the same complete quotient-graph and
swap procedure gives 15 actions. It retains the affine cocycle and dual
graph cases. It does not replace a proper subdirect product by the full
direct product.

For the 29 zero-ternary actions it first proves completeness of the
four-point subgroup list by closure under adjoining every permutation.
It constructs the two induced linear frames from their actual stabilizer
maps. Cyclic closure proves the complete invariant binary-kernel lists.
For every kernel it checks a presentation on the actual top generators,
computes the exact binary relator matrix for cocycles, and derives
`dim H1 = dim Z1 - dim(module) + dim(fixed space)`. Its literal complements
are pairwise nonconjugate and their number is `2^dim H1`; this proves
complement completeness. Conjugacy of complements under the whole
semidirect product agrees with conjugacy under its binary base, since the
group is the product of that base and either complement. The computation
retains 20 kernel/complement interfaces and 48 transitive lifts, yielding
29 permutation-conjugacy classes.

The construction file has exactly the fields `paired8`, `zero16`, and
`two_affine16`. Each contains one entry per action: `id`, literal
constructed `generators`, and a `conjugator`. With GAP's right-action
convention, the generated group raised to that conjugator must equal the
committed alphabet action. There are 68 such transporters. Replay rebuilds
the constructions independently of this file and verifies both complete
class correspondence and the retained literal transporters. Thus an
unexplained list of 24, 29, or 15 entries is not its coverage argument.

The twelve-point partition is 283 joint-capacity actions, eight with a
cyclic-three or symmetric-three quotient by their binary core, eight with
four ternary orbits, and two paired-six actions. The checker verifies every
exact width-24 predicate and the width-32 profile inequalities. It also
rejects the insufficient six-ordinary-character and unsharpened
four-linear/four-relative profiles.

The finite data establish the profiles for the supplied literal actions.
Completeness of the degree-12 transitive classification and the primitive
degree-8/16 classifications is an external mathematical input.
`--classifiers` checks correspondence with a GAP installation; neither
catalogue size nor successful correspondence proves those classifications.
Exact GAP character, automorphism, normalizer, conjugacy and presentation
algorithms are trusted computations; this is not a formal verification of
those implementations. The uniform reduction of arbitrary actions to those block structures,
Goursat's theorem, and the elementary induced-frame and cocycle arguments
remain mathematical inputs. The bounded cases and all lift choices in
those reductions are covered by `--constructors`.

For an arbitrary original action `U` on 24 or 32 points, its elementary pair
kernel may be proper or zero, its quartet kernel may be nonabelian, and a
normal quotient need not split. Passing from these top profiles to every
literal `N normal U` uses the universal relative-socle, quartet-kernel,
ternary-averaging, core, and natural-action lifting theorems. This finite
package does not substitute a list of sampled children for those theorems.
It does not compute an original action normalizer from a top normalizer,
or prove the complete weighted aggregate by itself.
