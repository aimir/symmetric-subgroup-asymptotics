\section{The exceptional small pair actions}
\label{sec:exceptional-pairs}
Put $\beta=\log_2(38/25)$ and $\gamma_5=\frac13\log_2 5$.
Throughout this section, a pair action means an actual transitive group
$U\leq C_2\wr S_s$ in its specified action on $2s$ points. Write
$E=U\cap C_2^s$, $T=U/E$, and $M_s=\F_2^s$ with its actual $T$ action.
Every normal subgroup mentioned below is a normal subgroup of this
original $U$, unless another group is specified.

\begin{lemma}[Relative socles and fixed prefixes]\label{lem:pair-relative}
Let $F\lhd U$ be a binary group and $S=U/F$. Suppose that, for every
$N\lhd U$, the multiplicity $m_X$ of each simple binary type $X$ in
$\Soc(U/N)\cap FN/N$ satisfies $m_X\leq q e_X$, where
$e_X=\dim_{\End_U X}X$. Suppose every quotient of $S$ has a faithful
tuple of at most $k$ irreducible characters. Then every $Q=U/N$ has a
faithful tuple consisting of a $k$-character prefix with joint binary
kernel, followed by $q$ general characters. Consequently
\[
 |\Epi(J,Q)|\leq |\Aut Q|[Q:O_2(Q)]^q
                   5^{kb/3}(38/25)^{qb}\qquad(J\leq S_b).
\]
If $Q$ is binary, the whole tuple can instead be counted in the known
binary-source mode. If a specified top quotient has a faithful tuple of
$r$ binary-primary linear characters, one may use those $r$ positions
and the same $q$ relative general positions.
For $F=E$, the relative hypothesis follows whenever every homogeneous
section $X^m$ of $M_s$ satisfies $m\leq q e_X$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $A=FN/N$. A minimal normal subgroup of $Q$ contained in $A$ is
central in $A$ and elementary abelian. Thus $V=\Soc(Q)\cap A$ is a
semisimple binary $Q/A$-module. The dual $V^*$ has $q$ module generators:
for $X^m$ the required number is $\lceil m/e_X\rceil$, and distinct
simple types share positions. Induce each corresponding linear
character of $V$ to $Q$ and choose an irreducible constituent. Its
restriction to $V$ consists of conjugates of that linear character;
hence the intersection of these $q$ kernels with $V$ is trivial.
Inflate a faithful tuple of $Q/A$. The joint kernel of the resulting
tuple is normal in $Q$, lies in $A$, and meets $\Soc(Q)$ trivially,
so it is trivial. This does not extend a character to a nonabelian
$A$ or split the original extension. Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor-count} to the fixed prefix. When $F=E$,
$V$ is a section of the original $M_s$. All choices depend on $Q$,
not on the later complete source $J$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A point-orbit generator bound]\label{lem:point-orbit-generators}
Let a group $T$ act on a finite set $\Omega$, and let $g\in T$ have
at most $c$ cycles on $\Omega$. Over any field $k$, every
$T$-invariant subspace of $k^\Omega$ is generated by at most $c$
vectors as a $k[T]$-module. The same bound holds for its quotient
modules. There is no restriction on the characteristic or the order
of $g$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $t$ act as $g$. One delta function from each cycle generates
$k^\Omega$ over $k[t]$. Pull an invariant subspace back along the
resulting surjection $k[t]^c\twoheadrightarrow k^\Omega$.
The pullback is free of rank at most $c$, since $k[t]$ is a principal
ideal domain. Mapping a basis down gives at most $c$ generators for
the subspace, first over $k[t]$ and hence over $k[T]$. Their images
generate every quotient.
\end{proof}

The same argument applies to any element $a\in k[T]$: if $k^\Omega$
is generated by $c$ vectors as a $k[t]$-module with $t$ acting as $a$,
then every $T$-invariant subquotient needs at most $c$ generators.
A finite certificate can give the seed vectors, successive applications
of $a$, and coefficients reconstructing every coordinate vector.
These identities prove generation directly and require no invariant-subspace
list. For the first zero-core sixteen-point action, one such group-algebra
operator gives the required four-generator bound.

\begin{lemma}[Normal generators for product and affine tops]
\label{lem:product-affine-normal-generators}
Suppose every normal subgroup of finite groups $G$ and $H$ is normally
generated by at most $a$ and $b$ elements, respectively. Then every normal
subgroup of $G\times H$ is normally generated by at most $a+b$ elements.
Suppose also that $V\ne0$ is a finite elementary abelian group and
$R\leq\operatorname{GL}(V)$ acts irreducibly. If every normal subgroup
of $R$ is normally generated by at most $t\geq1$ elements, the same bound
holds for every normal subgroup of $V\rtimes R$.
In either conclusion the bound also holds for the socle of every quotient.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $N\lhd G\times H$, use normal generators of
$N\cap(G\times1)$ and lifts in $N$ of normal generators of its image
in $H$. Their normal closure has the same intersection and image as $N$,
so it is $N$; this does not require $N$ to split as a product.

Every nontrivial normal subgroup $N$ of $V\rtimes R$ contains $V$.
Indeed, irreducibility makes $N\cap V$ either $0$ or $V$; in the first
case $[N,V]=1$, and faithfulness gives $N\leq C_{V\rtimes R}(V)=V$,
hence $N=1$. If $N=V$, any nonzero vector normally generates it.
Otherwise lift normal generators of the nontrivial image of $N$ in $R$.
Their normal closure is nontrivial, hence contains $V$, and therefore is $N$.
Finally, normal generators descend under surjections; apply this to the
preimage of each quotient socle.
\end{proof}

When the normal subgroups of a finite group form a chain, each is normally
cyclic: a largest normal closure of one of its elements contains every
other such closure. Thus the lemma also supplies a two-generator bound
for products of two finite groups with normal chains. These structural
arguments replace normal-subgroup enumeration whenever the literal top
has the displayed affine or product identification.

\begin{lemma}[Centralizers in an abelian affine model]
\label{lem:affine-translation-centralizer}
Let $V$ be an abelian group and $\varphi:H\to\operatorname{Aut}(V)$.
Projection onto $H$ induces an isomorphism
\[
 C_{V\rtimes_\varphi H}(V)/V\cong\ker\varphi.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An element $(v,h)$ commutes with every translation $(w,1)$ exactly when
$\varphi(h)w=w$ for every $w\in V$. The projection of the centralizer
is therefore $\ker\varphi$, and its kernel is $V$.
\end{proof}

For a literal affine identification, this reduces the centralizer-quotient
certificate to the action kernel in the complement. The complement action
on $V$ may have a nontrivial kernel; the permutation action on the original
points is retained separately. In the four-ternary models the complement
has order at most $64$, even when the full top has order $5184$.

\begin{lemma}[Two elementary module bounds]\label{lem:small-monomial}
A transitive monomial module of dimension $r>1$ over a field has no
homogeneous section $X^m$ with $m>\lfloor r/2\rfloor$.
For the regular binary $V_4$ module
$W=\F_2[x,y]/(x^2,y^2)$, every submodule has head dimension at most two;
the unique submodule attaining two is the augmentation ideal
$I=(x,y)$, of dimension three. These statements also hold after a
field extension.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A transitive permutation group has a derangement: the average number
of fixed points is one, and the identity fixes more than one point.
Choose a monomial element whose permutation is a derangement. Its
module over the principal ideal domain $k[t]$ is a sum of at most
$\lfloor r/2\rfloor$ cyclic modules, one per cycle. The pullback
argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:point-orbit-generators} gives the same
generator bound for every subquotient. On the other hand $X^m$, restricted
to this element, requires at least $m$ generators, by any nonzero
primary component and the invariant-factor theorem.
For the second assertion a proper ideal lies in $I$, whose radical
is $I^2=\langle xy\rangle$. A one-dimensional image in $I/I^2$
generates a cyclic ideal, whereas a two-dimensional image forces
the entire $I$. The whole ring has cyclic head. This proves the
claim and its equality assertion over any field of characteristic two.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The finite inputs and what they certify}
\label{subsec:pair-finite-input}
We use the following finite input on \emph{top actions}, separately
from the universal lifting arguments that follow. For a simple binary
$T$-module $X$, write $\delta_X=\dim_{\F_2}X$. Define
\[
 \tau(T)=\max_{B\leq M_s,\ X}
 \left\lceil\frac{\dim_{\F_2}\Hom_T(B,X)}{\delta_X}\right\rceil,
 \qquad \kappa(T)=\max_{L\lhd T}c(T/L),
\]
where $c$ denotes the least size of a faithful irreducible character
tuple. The formula for $\tau$ is exactly the largest Schur demand of a
homogeneous section: the multiplicity of $X$ in the head of $B$ is
$\dim\Hom_T(B,X)/\dim\End_T(X)$.

\begin{lemma}[Certified top data]\label{lem:small-pair-data}
The top-action certificates have the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Among the $301$ transitive degree-twelve actions, $283$ have
$(\tau,\kappa)$ equal to
\[
 (1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(2,1),(2,2),(3,1),
\]
with respective multiplicities $71,102,19,33,56,2$.
The remaining eighteen consist of eight binary-core actions with
quotient $C_3$ or $S_3$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:twelve-core}; eight actions
with an elementary normal ternary group on four triples as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:four-triple-compression}; and two paired actions with
six-point tops as in Lemma~\ref{lem:paired-six}.
\item For the two six-point groups
\[
 S_{36}=\langle(124),(24)(56),(13)(25)(46)\rangle,
 \quad
 S_{72}=\langle(124),(24),(13)(25)(46)\rangle,
\]
all homogeneous sections of $M_6$ have Schur demand at most one.
Their only simple types have dimensions one and four, with commuting
field $\F_2$. Every nontrivial quotient has a faithful irreducible
character except $C_2^2$, which has two faithful binary linear positions.
\item For all $24$ nonbinary transitive degree-eight groups $S$, every
nontrivial homogeneous type in $M_8$ has Schur demand at most one,
every trivial section has dimension at most two, and $\kappa(S)\leq2$.
If an invariant submodule has trivial head dimension two, it is a
unique five-dimensional submodule $B$. Every invariant $D\geq B$
satisfies $\dim D/(B+[D,S])\leq1$.
\item The $29$ zero-ternary minimal-four degree-sixteen tops, the $15$
degree-sixteen tops with two primitive affine eight-point blocks, and
the $20$ primitive affine degree-sixteen tops all satisfy
$\tau\leq4$ and $\kappa\leq2$.
The two inequivalent nontrivial binary composition factors of the
projective eight-point $\operatorname{PSL}_2(7)$ module have dimension
three, commuting field $\F_2$, and each occurs once; the trivial factor
occurs twice.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the sixty-four sixteen-point module bounds in (iv), use
Lemma~\ref{lem:point-orbit-generators} and its group-algebra variant.
Sixty-three literal actions have a displayed element with at most four
point orbits; the remaining action has the four-seed polynomial
certificate described above. These give $\tau\leq4$ for every invariant
subquotient directly.

The other finite calculations are recorded by the pair-top certificate
contract in Appendix~\ref{sec:certificates}. We specify their mathematical
coverage, since a list of tested module examples would not suffice.
Starting with every vector, form its cyclic submodule and close the
resulting list under sums. Every invariant subspace is the sum of
cyclic submodules of its vectors, so this constructs the complete
invariant-subspace lattice. Maximal intervals give the simple modules;
intertwining linear equations on the same generators identify their
isomorphism types, their endomorphism fields, and the displayed Hom
spaces. A composition chain gives the separate composition bounds.
Thus every numerator and every homogeneous quotient is covered.
One may also certify the nontrivial socle bounds without identifying all
simple types. For invariant $A\leq C\leq B$ with $[B,S]\leq C$,
the quotient $B/C$ is pointwise fixed. Every map from a nontrivial simple
module $X$ into $B/A$ therefore lands in $C/A$, so
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(X,B/A)=\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,C/A)
\]
under the natural inclusion. One can also remove a fixed lower layer:
if $[C,S]\leq A$, the projection $B/A\to B/C$ has fixed kernel $C/A$.
Postcomposition induces an injection
\[
 \operatorname{Hom}_S(X,B/A)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_S(X,B/C).
\]
Indeed, a nonzero map from the nontrivial simple module $X$ is injective,
so its image cannot lie in a pointwise fixed kernel. Thus removing either
kind of fixed layer does not decrease the bound on the nontrivial socle.
A remaining quotient with cyclic dual has Schur demand at most one;
the same holds for a binary quotient of dimension at most two when $X$
is nontrivial. These steps commute with surjective pullback of the acting
group. This certificate allows several trivial generators in the whole
dual while retaining the required nontrivial bound.
In the finite registry, it suffices to record for every nested pair a
reversed cyclic edge, a dimension jump at most two, or an intermediate
state removing a nonzero fixed upper or lower layer. Induction on
$\dim B-\dim A$ verifies every such descent with one uniform argument.
Vector-orbit completeness can be checked without enumerating the acting
group. For chosen representatives $v_i$, record for every vector $v$ an
index $i$ and a word $g$ in the retained generators such that $gv_i=v$.
An invariant submodule containing $v$ also contains $v_i=g^{-1}v$,
so the cyclic-span and join tables recover every invariant submodule.
For $M_8$ this uses only $256$ vector witnesses, including for the natural
$A_8$ and $S_8$ actions.
For normal subgroups start with kernels of all irreducible characters
and close under intersections. Every normal subgroup occurs, since
the regular representation of its quotient is faithful and decomposes
into irreducibles. Literal kernels, not quotient isomorphism types,
are retained. Searching these kernel intersections proves the asserted
covers for all quotients.
These quotient checks share one finite-table proof. For each normal kernel
$N$, the preimage of the quotient socle is the least normal subgroup $C$
containing $N$ and every normal subgroup minimal strictly above $N$.
A tuple $x_1,\ldots,x_t\in C$ normally generates $C/N$ in the ambient
quotient precisely when every normal subgroup containing $N$ and all the
$x_i$ also contains $C$.
Completeness of the normal registry makes both assertions finite inclusion
tests. The same argument then covers every quotient, with only the inclusion
and tuple-membership tables varying between actions.
Another shortcut is a uniform bound of $t$ ambient normal generators
for every normal subgroup of the top. Apply it to the preimage of a
quotient socle and map the tuple into the quotient. Such a uniform bound
can be checked by reaching every normal-registry state from the identity
in at most $t$ steps, each adjoining the normal closure of one class
representative.
A cyclic top needs no normal registry: each of its quotient socles is
cyclic. If its point action is regular, the polynomial-module argument
above also gives one generator for every invariant submodule over any
field. For the first twelve-point top, the two retained generators are
the third and fourth powers of the cycle $x\mapsto x+1\pmod {12}$;
the cycle itself is their quotient. These two identities and its single
point orbit therefore prove both one-position bounds directly.
There is a useful shortcut when the complete normal registry is a chain.
Every quotient again has a chain of normal subgroups, hence at most one
minimal nontrivial normal subgroup. If present, that subgroup is the socle
and any nonidentity element normally generates it; otherwise the socle is
trivial. Thus all quotient socles have a one-position normal-generating
tuple, without separate generator searches for the quotients.
For a faithful irreducible affine group $V\rtimes R$ with nontrivial simple
complement $R$, this chain is $1<V<V\rtimes R$. Indeed, a normal subgroup
meeting $V$ trivially centralizes $V$ and is therefore trivial; any other
normal subgroup contains $V$, and projection to $R$ gives the two remaining
possibilities. The literal degree-eight affine actions can thus be handled
by explicit binary coordinates and certificates for their complements of
orders $7$ and $168$, without enumerating the full affine groups.
More generally, a cover of the normal registry by $t$ chains gives a
$t$-position tuple for every quotient. The quotient correspondence
restricts the original cover to the normal subgroups above the quotient
kernel. Distinct minimal
normal subgroups are incomparable, so each chain contains at most one of
them. Choose a nonidentity element in each occupied chain, and the identity
in each empty chain. The chosen elements normally generate all minimal
normal subgroups and hence the socle. Thus a single finite chain-cover
certificate can replace all quotient-specific socle-generator searches.
These structural bounds also supply the finite-registry certificates:
lift a normally generating tuple for the quotient socle to the original
group and take its normal closure together with the quotient kernel.
By the subgroup correspondence this is exactly the relative socle carrier
$C$. Thus the same registry interface accepts either an explicit finite
tuple certificate or a structural proof, without changing any quotient
kernel or adding a counting assumption.

Completeness of the degree-twelve and primitive action lists is the
published classification input
\cite{Hulpke2005,HoltRoyle2020,RoneyDougal2005Primitive,TransGrp365,PrimGrp344}.
The imprimitive degree-eight and two-affine-block constructions use
all normal quotients of the local group, all graph isomorphisms between
these quotients, and all block-swap cosets in their actual normalizers;
Goursat's lemma and the order-two coset condition prove completeness.
The four-pair constructions use the full invariant-kernel list and all
lifts of fixed top generators. The zero-ternary list similarly uses
all degree-four local subgroups, both induced binary linear frames,
all invariant kernels, and every solution of the generator-relator
cocycle equations. The computed cocycle-space dimension, modulo the
coboundary-space dimension, verifies that every complement class occurs.
Transporters identify actions in the stated lists. These procedures
certify only the finite top data. In particular they make no assertion
about unlisted normal subgroups of a subsequent pair lift.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Natural binary modules]\label{lem:natural-binary-cyclic}
Let $n\geq4$ be even and let $A_n\leq T\leq S_n$ act on
$M=\F_2^n$ by permuting coordinates. Every $T$-invariant subspace
of $M$ is generated by the orbit of one vector.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a vector $v$ is nonconstant, choose three coordinates on which
its values are not all equal. Adding $v$ to its image under the
three-cycle on these coordinates gives a vector of weight two.
The alternating group is transitive on pairs, so the orbit span
of $v$ contains every vector $e_i+e_j$, and hence the even-sum
hyperplane $I$.

Now let $W\leq M$ be invariant. If $W$ contains an odd-sum vector
$v$, then $v$ is nonconstant because $n$ is even. Its orbit span
contains $I$ and $v$, and is therefore all of $M=W$. If every
vector in $W$ has even sum but some vector is nonconstant, the
same argument gives $W=I$ generated by that vector. Otherwise
$W$ consists of constant vectors and has dimension at most one.
This proves the assertion, including $W=0$. In particular the
natural $A_{16}$ and $S_{16}$ rows need no invariant-vector census.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The twelve-pair actions}

\begin{lemma}[A binary-primary prefix]\label{lem:binary-primary-prefix}
Let $Q$ be finite and put $V=\Omega_1(Z(Q))_2$. If
$\dim V\leq r+1$ and $\dim(V\cap Q')\leq r$, there is a linear
character $\chi$ of binary-power order with
$\dim(V\cap\ker\chi)\leq r$.
If $\psi$ is a linear character of odd order with binary kernel,
then $\chi\psi$ has binary kernel and the same residual central
binary subspace as $\chi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\dim V\leq r$, take the trivial character. Otherwise choose
$z\in V\setminus Q'$. Characters of the finite abelianization
separate points, so some linear character detects $z$. Raising
that character to the odd part of $|Q|$ gives binary-power order
and still detects $z$. Its kernel on $V$ therefore has codimension
one. This uses full-order characters: a sign character need not
detect the square of an element of order four.

Binary-power and odd-order roots of unity cannot cancel, so
$\ker(\chi\psi)=\ker\chi\cap\ker\psi$. Moreover $\psi$ is
trivial on every involution. This proves both assertions about
the twist. For either prefix, the ambient-fixed part of the
kernel's central binary socle injects into the corresponding
residual subspace of $V$. Thus the same bound applies to its
trivial simple type; the nontrivial simple types must still be
bounded on that actual kernel.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[An anchor when the odd part is three]\label{lem:ternary-odd-part-anchor}
Let $|Q|=2^a3$. Every jointly faithful tuple of irreducible characters
of $Q$ contains a character with binary kernel. Thus a faithful tuple
of $r+1$ characters supplies one ordinary prefix and $r$ relative
positions on its binary kernel, using the same whole-group characters.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A nonbinary normal subgroup contains a Sylow three subgroup of $Q$,
and normality makes it contain every conjugate. If every character
kernel were nonbinary, their intersection would therefore contain a
fixed nontrivial Sylow three subgroup, contradicting faithfulness.
Choose a binary-kernel member as the prefix and retain the other
$r$ characters as the tail. For each tail character, Maschke supplies
an irreducible constituent on the actual prefix kernel; the normal
restriction argument in Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-relative} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The binary-core twelve-point tops]\label{lem:twelve-core}
Let $T$ be one of the eight binary-core actions in
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-pair-data}(i). Put $P=O_2(T)$.
For every pair lift $U$ and every $N\lhd U$, put $Q=U/N$.
Every nonbinary $Q$ has a socle normally generated by at most four
elements and admits four general faithful positions, one of which has
binary kernel. Thus it has one ordinary anchor and three relative
general positions. Every binary $Q$ admits one binary-primary linear
and four relative general positions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In all eight actions the original group $U\leq S_{24}$ has order
$2^a3$, is solvable, and has only semiregular nonidentity elements
of order three. The literal geometry supplies these facts directly.
The unique $C_3$ case has
\[
 T=P\rtimes\langle h\rangle,\qquad
 P=\{(a,b,a+b):a,b\in V_4\}\leq V_4^3,
\]
where $P$ acts by translations on three four-point blocks and $h$
cycles the blocks. In particular $|T|=48$. For the $S_3$ cases,
the literal product chart or an equivariant tower
$\Omega_{12}\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_6\twoheadrightarrow\Omega_3$
with two-point fibres gives a full $S_3$ image with binary kernel.
A nonidentity element of order three survives on the three blocks
and fixes no point. The pair lift has binary kernel as well, so this
semiregularity holds on the original twenty-four points. Generator
identities and short covering words certify the displayed actions;
no normal-subgroup list of the pair lift is used.

First let $Q$ be nonbinary, and write $\pi:U\twoheadrightarrow Q$
for the original quotient map. Then $N$ is binary and $|Q|=2^b3$.
Fix $h\in U$ of order three. For any central
elementary binary subgroup $A\leq Q$, coprime centralizer lifting maps
$C_U(h)\cap\pi^{-1}(A)$ onto $A$. The kernel of the action on the
eight $h$-orbits lies in the odd group $C_3^8$, so it is killed by
the map to $A$. Thus $A$ is a binary quotient of a subgroup of $S_8$,
and has rank at most four by the permutation prime-rank bound.
Every elementary binary section of $Q$ has rank at most twelve,
using the original faithful action of $U$ on twenty-four points.
Put $V=\Soc(Q)_2$. The fixed type in $V$ therefore has multiplicity
at most four. A nonfixed simple type $X$, with binary dimension
$\delta_X$ and Schur dimension $e_X$, satisfies $\delta_Xm_X\leq12$.
If its Schur product degree $\delta_Xe_X$ is at least three, then
$m_X\leq4e_X$. Only product degree two needs further attention;
then $\delta_X=2$, $\End_Q(X)=\F_4$, and $e_X=1$.

When $T/P=S_3$, this last type cannot occur. Indeed, $U$, and hence
$Q$, has no quotient of order three: such a map kills the binary
kernel of $U\twoheadrightarrow S_3$ and would descend to $S_3$.
A simple binary type of product degree two gives precisely such
a scalar quotient of order three.

For the $C_3$ action, we prove the needed bound on the actual module
$M_{12}$. Let $B\leq M_{12}$ be $T$-invariant and put
$d=\dim B/[B,P]$. Each four-point coordinate is a regular $V_4$
module. By Lemma~\ref{lem:small-monomial}, every local submodule $S$
has head dimension at most two, with equality only for its
three-dimensional augmentation ideal. In particular
\[
 3\dim S/[S,P]\leq\dim S+3.
\]
Applying the head inequality along the three-coordinate filtration
gives $d\leq6$ and $3d\leq\dim B+9$: head dimension is subadditive
in exact sequences, while ordinary dimension is additive. If $d=6$,
the kernel of
each coordinate projection has head dimension at most four, so every
coordinate image is its augmentation ideal $I_i$. Hence
$B\leq I_1\oplus I_2\oplus I_3$ and $\dim B\leq9$, while the
weighted inequality gives $\dim B\geq9$. Thus the original numerator
is exactly this augmentation sum. The $T$-orbits of two augmentation
generators supported on one block span this sum.

Now let $X$ be the actual simple target of product degree two.
The normal binary subgroup $P$ fixes $X$, so every $T$-map $B\to X$
factors through the original coinvariant module $B/[B,P]$.
This is a semisimple $C_3$-module. Its Hom space to $X$ has even
binary dimension, by postcomposition with $\End_T(X)=\F_4$, and
has dimension at most $d$. Thus $d\leq5$ gives
$\dim\Hom_T(B,X)\leq4$. When $d=6$, the two generators just
constructed give the same bound $2\dim X=4$.
For any homogeneous section $X^m$, pull its numerator back to the
original $M_{12}$. Precomposition embeds
$\Hom_T(X^m,X)$ into the Hom space of that numerator, so $2m\leq4$.
This proves $m\leq2$ for every such section, with its original action.

Filter $V$ by $V\cap EN/N$ and its image in $Q/(EN/N)$.
The first term embeds in the actual pair section $E/(E\cap N)$
of $M_{12}$. Pulling the same simple $Q$-type back to $U$ and
descending along $U\twoheadrightarrow T$ gives multiplicity at most
two there. The second term is elementary binary in a quotient of the
literal top of order $48$, so has dimension at most four and contains
at most two copies of that same type. Multiplicities add along this
filtration, giving $m_X\leq4=4e_X$. No quotient is replaced by
an abstract isomorphic group.

In both top cases every binary socle type now has demand at most four.
The odd socle has order at most three and shares the binary generator
positions; solvability excludes nonabelian minimal normal subgroups.
Thus the whole socle needs at most four ambient normal generators.
The whole-socle case of Theorem~\ref{thm:character-feasibility} gives
four faithful general positions, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ternary-odd-part-anchor} selects a binary-kernel member
of this same tuple. This is profile $(0,0,1,3)$ for both $C_3$ and
$S_3$ tops.

Finally let $Q$ be binary, for either top case. A Sylow three subgroup
$\langle h\rangle\leq N$ has eight three-point orbits. Frattini gives
$U=N N_U(\langle h\rangle)$. In the ambient normalizer
$C_3^8:(C_2\times S_8)$, the common inversion factor $C_2$ must be
retained. The odd base of the actual normalizer is killed by $N$;
its actual joint image $H\leq C_2\times S_8$ surjects onto $Q$.
Retaining this joint image preserves the correlations between inversion
and orbit permutation. Its faithful ten-point action bounds every
elementary binary section by five. Since the inversion factor is
abelian, $H'$ acts faithfully on the eight orbit points, so every
elementary binary section of $H'$, and hence of $Q'$, has rank at most
four. Thus the central binary rank of $Q$ is at most five and its
derived central rank at most four. Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-primary-prefix}
gives a binary-primary linear character $\chi$ with
$\dim(\Omega_1(Z(Q))\cap\ker\chi)\leq4$.
Since all simple binary $Q$-types are trivial, the $Q$-socle of
$\Omega_1(Z(\ker\chi))$ is precisely this ambient-fixed subspace.
The relative-socle construction supplies four general positions on
the actual kernel.
This is profile $(1,0,0,4)$ in the known binary-source mode.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Four ternary orbits]\label{lem:four-triple-compression}
Suppose $T$ has an elementary normal ternary subgroup $C$ of rank at
least two with four orbits of size three, $T/C$ binary, and
$|O_2(T)|\leq2$. Every pair lift $U$ has a quotient $R$ with a
specified faithful twenty-point action and a constant $D_U$ such that
\[
 Z_J(U)\leq Z_J(R)+D_U5^b\qquad(J\leq S_b).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Averaging over $C$ gives the $T$-equivariant decomposition
$M_{12}=M_0\oplus W$, with $\dim M_0=4$, $\dim W=8$.
The map $(v,t)\mapsto(\operatorname{avg}_C(v),t)$ on the ambient
semidirect product has kernel $W$. Restricted to $U$, its kernel is
$E_1=E\cap W=[E,C]$; put $R=U/E_1$. The ambient $M_0:T$ acts
faithfully on the disjoint union of the twelve pair labels and the
eight $C$-orbit labels on the original twenty-four points. The first
action detects $T$ and the second detects $M_0$, proving faithfulness
of this twenty-point representation after restriction to $R$.

Fix an original $N$ not containing $E_1$, and choose a minimal normal
$S$ in the nonzero image of $E_1$ in $Q=U/N$. On restriction to $C$,
$W$ consists of four nontrivial two-dimensional local types. Their
kernels have trivial intersection, and they form one $T$-orbit.
There are at least two distinct types, since $C$ has rank at least two;
each has multiplicity at most two. Every nonzero $T$-section of $W$
therefore acts faithfully on $C$. In particular if $L$ is the kernel
of the action of $T$ on $S$, then $L\cap C=1$, so $L$ is normal binary
and $L\leq O_2(T)$.

Put $A=EN/N$. Since $A$ is abelian, there is an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow C_Q(S)
 \longrightarrow L/\pi(N)\longrightarrow1.
\]
Every minimal normal subgroup of $Q$ commutes with $S$, including
$S$ itself. Thus the entire socle lies in this binary centralizer.
Its image modulo $A$ has dimension at most one and is trivial.
The trivial part in $A$ is a section of $M_0$, the ordinary permutation
module on four blocks, so has rank at most two by
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-monomial}. Every nontrivial type in $A$ lies in
$W$ and has multiplicity at most two by restriction to $C$.
The whole socle therefore has Schur demand at most three, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:character-feasibility} supplies three general positions.
Its epimorphisms cost at most $|\Aut Q|5^b$.
Finally the interval $N\geq E_1$ is exactly the normal-subgroup lattice
of $R$, with the same quotients and epimorphism sets. Summing that
interval gives exactly $Z_J(R)$; summing the other normals gives
the asserted constant $D_U=\sum_{N\not\supseteq E_1}|\Aut(U/N)|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The paired six-point tops]\label{lem:paired-six}
Suppose $U\to T\to S$ has degrees $24,12,6$, with both successive
systems consisting of pairs, and $S=S_{36}$ or $S_{72}$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-pair-data}. Every original quotient admits either
one general anchor and three relative general positions, or two
binary-primary linears and three relative general positions in the
known binary-source mode. A binary quotient with no exceptional
$C_2^2$ top needs at most four general positions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $F$ be the kernel of $U\to S$. On the six original four-point
blocks, $F\leq D_8^6$, and $F/E$ is a submodule of $M_6$.
Let $C_0\leq M_{12}$ be the vectors constant on the six pairs of
pair labels. Both $C_0$ and $M_{12}/C_0$ are $M_6$.
Fix $N$, put $A=FN/N$, and take a homogeneous component $X^m$ of
$\Soc(U/N)\cap A$. Its preimage $D\leq F$ has the three successive
factors obtained from
\[
 D\cap E\cap C_0,\qquad
 (D\cap E+C_0)/C_0,\qquad \pi(D)\leq F/E.
\]
The denominators are the respective images of $D\cap N$; the last
is $\pi(F\cap N)$, since $D$ contains $F\cap N$.
Thus all three factors are homogeneous sections of the same $M_6$
for the same original $S$ type. Their multiplicities add, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:small-pair-data}(ii) gives $m\leq3e_X$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-relative}. Except for top $C_2^2$, a
nontrivial top quotient has a one-character prefix with binary joint
kernel. For top $C_2^2$, $Q$ itself is binary and the prefix consists
of two full-order binary linear characters. A trivial top needs only
the three relative positions. No part of this proof assumes that
$F$ or $A$ is abelian.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Complete twelve-pair estimate]\label{thm:twelve-pairs}
The family of all actual pair actions with twelve pairs and nonbinary
top has a complete exponentially small forward row and a
quadratic-exponentially small scalar, with its original action weights.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the $283$ generic tops, Lemma~\ref{lem:pair-relative} gives slope
$\kappa\gamma_5+\tau\beta$ and target-dependent finite constants.
For each of the six pairs in Lemma~\ref{lem:small-pair-data}(i),
\[
 5^{64\kappa}38^{192\tau}<2^{573}25^{192\tau},
\]
so the slope is less than $3-1/64$. The binary-core slopes are
$\frac12+4\beta$ and $\gamma_5+3\beta$, both less than $3-1/64$.
The paired-six slopes
are $\gamma_5+3\beta$, $1+3\beta$, and $4\beta$, with the same
strict margin. These inequalities follow, without rounding logarithms,
by raising to the $192$nd power; the corresponding numerators are
$2^{96}38^{768}$, $5^{64}38^{576}$,
$2^{192}38^{576}$, and $38^{768}$, against $2^{573}$ times the
matching power of $25$. Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor-count} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:character-forward} sum every literal normal in these
finite original width-twenty-four actions. Constants such as
$[Q:O_2(Q)]^q|\Aut Q|$ are fixed before choosing $J$.
For the other eight tops, Lemma~\ref{lem:four-triple-compression}
and Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-comparator} apply with $v=20<24$ and
$\eta=\log_2 5<3$. This last assertion includes the entire normal
interval of the quotient, rather than only its faithful target.
There are finitely many subgroups of $S_{24}$, so the complete union
is covered by one fusion, retaining the factor
$1/(b!a(U)p_nG_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor})$ in each original hot sum.
\end{proof}
