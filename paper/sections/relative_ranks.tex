\section{Relative heads of actual transitive actions}\label{sec:relative-ranks}
For $N\lhd A$ and a prime $p$, write
\[
 h_{p,A}(N)=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}N/(N^p[N,A]),\qquad
 \rho_A(N)=h_{2,A}(N),\quad \delta_A(N)=h_{3,A}(N).
\]
The distinction between a relative head and the abelianization of an
abstract isomorphic group is essential. For $B\leq C$ normal in $A$,
the quotient word maps onto the corresponding quotient word downstairs;
its kernel is an image of the head of $B$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-head-chain}
 h_{p,A}(C)\leq h_{p,A}(B)+h_{p,A/B}(C/B).
\end{equation}
If $G\leq\prod_{i=1}^t A_i$ is full on its actual orbit actions, filter
$G$ by the kernels of projection onto coordinates $i+1,\ldots,t$.
The image of the $i$th factor is a normal subgroup $N_i\lhd A_i$.
Conjugation on that factor is exactly the action of $A_i$, since the
coordinate projection of $G$ is onto. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit-relative-head}
 d_p(G)\leq\sum_i h_{p,A_i}(N_i).
\end{equation}
This assertion does not add the ranks of the full coordinate images.

\begin{theorem}[Binary first head]\label{thm:first-head}
For a faithful transitive $U\leq S_w$, $w\geq2$, and $N\lhd U$,
\[
 \rho_U(N)\leq3w/8,
\]
except for the whole-action pairs $(U,U)$ of the four critical actions
in Table~\ref{tab:critical}. Their heads have dimensions $1,2,2,4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We use strong induction on $w$. Tracey's induced-module theorem
\cite[Theorem~1.6]{Tracey2018} gives, for a submodule of an induced
binary module with fibre dimension $a$ and index $q\geq2$, at most
$aE(q,2)\leq aq/2$ module generators. Indeed, if $q$ has odd part
greater than one, the largest-prime-power branch is at most $q/3$;
if $q=2^k$, the other branch is
$\lfloor\sqrt{2/\pi}\,q/\sqrt{k}\rfloor$, which is at most $q/2$
for $k\geq3$ and equals $1,2$ for $k=1,2$.

Suppose first that $N$ is intransitive, with $q\geq2$ orbits of size
$m$. Let $K$ be the kernel of $U$ on these orbits and let $H$ be its
transitive action on one of them. The faithful embedding of $K$ in
$H^q$ and its coordinate-kernel filtration give
\[
 \rho_U(N)\leq\rho_K(N)\leq\sum_{i=1}^q\rho_H(E_i),
 \qquad E_i\lhd H.
\]
If $H$ is not critical, induction suffices. In each critical action
every proper normal subgroup has relative head at most $3m/8$:
for $C_2,V_4$ this is immediate; in $D_8$, squares and commutators
kill its central involution in each proper noncentral normal subgroup;
in $E_8$, every noncentral normal subgroup has commutator with $E_8$
equal to its centre and has image of dimension at most three modulo
that centre. These observations also suffice if the common image of
$N$ on an orbit is proper in $H$.

The remaining case is a subdirect $N\leq H^q$ with $H$ critical.
The block stabilizer induces the actual local automorphisms on every
characteristic elementary section of $H$; the sum of its conjugate
sections is its induced module of index $q$. For $C_2,V_4$ the preceding
module bound gives $q/2,q$. For $D_8,E_8$, use both
$N\cap Z(H)^q$ and its image in $(H/Z(H))^q$. Their fibre dimensions
are respectively $(1,2)$ and $(1,4)$, so
\eqref{eq:relative-head-chain} gives $3q/2,5q/2$, respectively.
All four bounds are at most $3mq/8$. In particular the central line
of a dihedral block has not been omitted.

If $N$ is transitive, then
\[
 \rho_U(N)\leq d(N)\leq
 \left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt3\,w}{2\sqrt{\log_2w}}\right\rfloor
\]
by \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Tracey2021Sharp}. This is at most $3w/8$ for
$w\geq38$, and also for
\[w\in\{16,19,24,27,30,32,33,35,36\}.\]
The remaining finite input checks the whole heads in the complete
transitive catalogues of degrees
\[
 2,3,\ldots,23,25,26,28,29,31,34,37.
\]
Its only failures are the four critical actions. The catalogue
classification is that of \cite{HoltRoyle2020,TransGrp365}; this slice
has 7,259 action classes. A separate enumeration of all 233 transitive
normal pairs in degrees $2,4,8$ shows that a critical transitive $N$
has a head above $3w/8$ only when $U=N$ is the corresponding critical
action. Thus the whole-head test is applied to the actual action of
$N$, and the separate pair test handles its possible overgroups.
The finite calculation constructs $N/(N^2[N,U])$ and verifies its
elementary binary invariants; its precise bounded contract is recorded
in Appendix~\ref{sec:certificates}. Together with the preceding
induction this proves the assertion in all degrees.
\end{proof}

We next give the relative ternary bounds needed for the one-triple
case. Let $\mathcal E_9$ consist of the four transitive actions
$9T2,9T6,9T7,9T17$, of orders $9,27,27,81$.

\begin{lemma}[Ordered-transversal head bound]\label{lem:ordered-transversal-head}
Let $P$ be a finite $p$-group, $H\leq P$, $[P:H]=p^t$, and let
$V$ be an $a$-dimensional representation of the original $H$ over a
field $k$. For every actual submodule $M\leq\operatorname{Ind}_H^P V$,
\[
 \dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_P(M,k)\leq
 a\,\operatorname{width}(\{0,\ldots,p-1\}^t).
\]
The order defining width is componentwise. For $p=3$ its width is at
most $W(t)$, where
\[
 W(0)=W(1)=1,\qquad W(2)=3,\qquad
 W(t)=7\cdot3^{t-3}\quad(t\geq3).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First choose an actual chain
$H=H_0\lhd H_1\lhd\cdots\lhd H_t=P$ with each index $p$.
For existence, a proper subgroup $L<P$ acts on $P/L$. The number of
fixed cosets is positive and divisible by $p$, since $[P:L]$ is
and all nontrivial orbit sizes are. A fixed coset $gL$ satisfies
$g^{-1}Lg\leq L$, hence equality by finiteness. Thus $L$ is
properly contained in its normalizer. An element of order $p$ in
$N_P(L)/L$ supplies the next subgroup of index $p$. Iteration and
index multiplication give exactly $t$ steps. Only consecutive
normality is required; $H$ need not be normal in $P$.

Choose $x_i\in H_i$ generating $H_i/H_{i-1}$, and set
$T(b)=x_1^{b_1}\cdots x_t^{b_t}$ for $0\leq b_i<p$.
Successively extracting the highest digit proves that these are unique
representatives for the original right cosets of $H$. Order the labels
by $L(b)=\sum_i b_ip^{i-1}$, so the largest differing coordinate decides
the comparison. If $d<b$ in this order and $b\leq c$ componentwise,
then the coset of $T(d)T(b)^{-1}T(c)$ has label strictly below $c$.
Indeed, at the largest differing coordinate $i$, the equal higher tails
of $d,b$ cancel. With $A,B,C\in H_{i-1}$ the remaining expression is
\[
 A x_i^{d_i-b_i}B^{-1}C x_i^{c_i}S_c
   =D x_i^{d_i-b_i+c_i}S_c,\qquad D\in H_{i-1}.
\]
This uses only $H_{i-1}\lhd H_i$. The exponent satisfies
$0\leq d_i-b_i+c_i<c_i<p$, so no carry changes a higher coordinate.
The lower coordinates absorb every commutator and stabilizer term.

Use the coinduced model
\[
 \{f:P\to V:f(hx)=\rho(h)f(x)\},\qquad
 (g\cdot f)(x)=f(xg).
\]
It is the original induced representation: $g\otimes v$ corresponds
to the function supported on $Hg^{-1}$ with value $v$ at $g^{-1}$;
the tensor relation gives precisely the displayed $\rho(h)$ twist.
Values at the $T(b)$ give one copy of the original $V$ per coset.
For $q=T(b)^{-1}T(c)$, act by $q^{-1}$. Then
\[
 (q^{-1}\cdot f)(T(c))=f(T(c)q^{-1})=f(T(b)).
\]
Thus the entire leading fibre vector moves unchanged from row $b$ to
row $c$. Every lower row moves to a row below $c$ by the preceding
calculation. Its original fibre twist is retained; it cannot create a
higher row. In particular this argument does not require an invariant
basis or a split fibre representation.

Choose an ordered basis of $V$ and order full labels $(b,j)$ by
$aL(b)+j$, with coset position first. Work in the intrinsic quotient
\[
 Q=M/\langle g\cdot u-u:g\in P,\ u\in M\rangle_k.
\]
Among lifts in $M$ of all bases of $Q$, minimize the sum of their
leading full-label heights. Such lifts exist, and the sum is a natural
number. If two different lifts have leading labels $(b,j),(c,j)$ with
$b\leq c$ componentwise, subtract from the second the scalar multiple
of $q^{-1}$ applied to the first which cancels its leading coefficient.
The new lift has strictly smaller height: the leading fibre row is
unchanged by transport, and every other term stays below $(c,j)$.
In $Q$ its class is the old second class minus that multiple of the
first, so the classes still form a basis and the new lift is nonzero.
This contradicts minimality. The case $b=c$ uses $q=1$ and also
excludes duplicate labels. Thus the leading labels inject into $a$
disjoint copies of the grid, with an antichain in each copy.
Invariant forms on $M$ are exactly $Q^*$, proving the dimension bound.
The quotient here is intrinsic to $M$, not its image in ambient
coinvariants.

For three ternary coordinates the following seven chains partition all
27 labels:
\[
\begin{array}{l}
000,100,200,210,220,221,222;\qquad001,101,201,211,212;\\
002,102,202;\qquad010,110,120,121,122;\qquad011,111,112;\\
012;\qquad020,021,022.
\end{array}
\]
Fixing the other $t-3$ coordinates gives $3^{t-3}$ slices, each met
at most seven times by an antichain. For $t=2$ use the chains
$00,10,20,21,22$; $01,11,12$; and $02$. The cases $t=0,1$ are immediate.
\end{proof}

For a positive integer $s$, write $s_3=3^t$ and define the integer
ternary envelope
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ternary-B}
 B(s)=\min\{C(s),s/\operatorname{lpp}(s/s_3)\},\qquad
 C(s)=\begin{cases}
 s,&t=0,\\
 s/3,&t=1,2,\\
 7s/27,&t\geq3,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{lpp}(1)=1$. We use this envelope directly in the
relative-head recurrence.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive inputs and block filtration]\label{lem:ternary-block}
For a primitive action $R$ of degree $r$, let $a_3(R)$ count its
composition factors of order three, with multiplicity. Then
\[
 a_3(R)\leq r/3,
\]
with equality only for the natural $C_3,S_3$ actions and the affine
degree-nine actions of orders $216,432$. Outside these four actions,
$a_3(R)<3r/10$. Every primitive normal pair has
$\delta_R(N)<3r/20$, apart from the whole natural $C_3,A_4$ pairs.
For a transitive $A$ with a minimal block system of $s$ blocks of size
$r$, primitive block component $R$, top $T$, and block kernel $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ternary-block-recurrence}
 \delta_A(N)\leq a_3(R)B(s)+\delta_T(NK/K).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The primitive generator bound
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{HoltRoneyDougal2013} gives
$\delta_R(N)\leq\lfloor\log_2r\rfloor$; its exceptional natural
$S_3$ case has zero relative ternary head. This proves the strict
$3r/20$ bound for $r\geq34$: check $34\leq r\leq63$, and then
use $20j<3\cdot2^j$ for $j\geq6$. The composition-length bound
\cite[Theorem~1.3]{GlasbyPraegerRosaVerret2018} gives
$a_3(R)\leq(8/3)\log_2r-4/3<3r/10$ for $r\geq45$.
At $45$ this follows from $45^{16}<2^{89}$, and the difference
is increasing thereafter. The bounded inputs enumerate all primitive
normal pairs through degree 33 and all primitive composition series
through degree 44. These are respectively 945 normal pairs in 253
actions and 336 actions, with exactly the exceptions stated above.
The weaker $r/3$ assertion follows immediately, including those
exceptions. Classification completeness uses
\cite{RoneyDougal2005Primitive,PrimGrp344}; the finite computations
count order-three composition factors, rather than the ternary
valuation of group order.

Here is the common ternary head bound used in the block argument.
Write $s_3=3^t$. Restrict to a Sylow $3$-subgroup and decompose the
original induced module by its actual double cosets, retaining the
twisted fibre actions. Its orbit indices are $3^j$ with $j\geq t$,
and their sum is $s$. Filter the given submodule by the kernels of
these actual coordinate maps. Head subadditivity and
Lemma~\ref{lem:ordered-transversal-head} bound each resulting head
by $aW(j)$. For $t=1,2$, all $W(j)\leq3^j/3$; for $t\geq3$,
all $W(j)=7\cdot3^j/27$. Summation therefore gives $aC(s)$,
including mixed orbit sizes. For $t=0$ the ambient dimension $as$
suffices. Restriction can only increase the invariant character head.

For a prime $q\ne3$, restrict instead to a Sylow $q$-subgroup.
Maschke's theorem makes every submodule of each induced component
a direct summand. Its maps to the trivial line therefore extend to
the component; Frobenius reciprocity bounds their dimension by $a$.
Each actual orbit has size at least $s_q$, so there are at most
$s/s_q$ components. Maximizing $s_q$ gives
$as/\operatorname{lpp}(s/s_3)$; at $s/s_3=1$ use the ambient
dimension $as$ instead.
Together these give $aB(s)$. All local coefficients and their sums
are integers; no floor is moved through the original fibre dimension.

For the block assertion, choose a chief series $R_i$ of $R$ and
filter $N\cap K$ by the inverse images of $R_i$ under all original
conjugate block evaluations. These maps separate the block kernel.
Each elementary layer is a submodule of the actual module induced
from a block stabilizer. A ternary layer of fibre dimension $a_i$
therefore contributes at most $a_iB(s)$; a layer at another prime
has zero ternary head.

For a nonabelian chief factor $Q$, each coordinate image of the
corresponding layer is normal in the original quotient of $R$,
hence either trivial or all of $Q$. Minimal normality makes $Q$
perfect. Choose a maximal proper normal subgroup $M\lhd Q$.
The ambient conjugates of the maps $Q\to Q/M$ are jointly injective,
since their common kernel is ambient-normal and proper in $Q$.
They give surjections onto simple perfect groups. Thus the original
layer, including any proper subdirect correlations, is a subdirect
product of simple perfect groups and is perfect: induct on the
number of coordinates, whose successive kernels are either trivial
or a full simple factor. Its ternary head is zero.

Sum this single chain using \eqref{eq:relative-head-chain}.
The relative second isomorphism theorem identifies the final top
section with $NK/K$ under the original ambient conjugation. The
ternary fibre dimensions sum to $a_3(R)$, proving
\eqref{eq:ternary-block-recurrence}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ternary relative stability]\label{thm:rank3-stability}
Except for the whole natural $C_3,A_4$ pairs and the four whole
$\mathcal E_9$ pairs, every transitive normal pair of degree $w$ obeys
\[
 \delta_A(N)\leq\lfloor5w/27\rfloor.
\]
Consequently the bounds $w/4$ and $2w/9$ hold outside, respectively,
the whole natural $C_3$ pair and the two whole natural pairs.
Positive equality in the former occurs only at natural $A_4$;
equality in the latter occurs precisely at the four whole
$\mathcal E_9$ pairs.
For an arbitrary $G\leq S_m$ with $c$ actual regular $C_3$ orbits
and $a$ actual natural $A_4$ orbits,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank3-pattern}
 9d_3(G)\leq2m+3c+a.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The finite primitive input in Lemma~\ref{lem:ternary-block} already
implies the assertion in the primitive case. The finite transitive
normal-pair input in degree nine says that the only two-ninth
equalities are the four whole $\mathcal E_9$ pairs and every other
pair has head at most one. This is a complete finite catalogue,
not an assumption on possible block systems.

From \eqref{eq:ternary-B}, $B(s)\leq s/3$ for $s\geq3$ and
\[
 B(2),B(3),B(4),B(6),B(9),B(18)=1,1,1,2,3,6.
\]
For $3\nmid s$ and $s\geq3$, the prime-to-three denominator is
at least four; the other cases follow from $C(s)$.
Use induction in \eqref{eq:ternary-block-recurrence}. For an ordinary
top with $s\geq3$ and $r\geq3$,
\[
 \delta_A(N)\leq rs/9+5s/27\leq5rs/27.
\]
For $r=2$ there is no ternary layer. At $s=2$ the top head vanishes
and $\delta_A(N)\leq a_3(R)\leq r/3\leq10r/27$.
For a whole $\mathcal E_9$ top the bound is
$3a_3(R)+2\leq r+2\leq5r/3$ for $r\geq3$;
$r=2$ is immediate. A natural $A_4$ top gives $a_3(R)+1$,
which is at most $20r/27$ for $r\geq3$; again $r=2$ is immediate.
A natural $C_3$ top also gives $a_3(R)+1$. Use the degree-nine
input at $r=3$, $a_3(R)\leq1$ at $r=4$, and
$r/3+1\leq5r/9$ for $r\geq5$; the binary-block case is immediate.
These cases exhaust the induction. Integrality gives the stated floors.

The coarser conclusions now follow by comparing $5/27<2/9<1/4$
and inspecting the six stated exceptions. Apply the two-ninth bound
to \eqref{eq:orbit-relative-head}; a natural $C_3$ layer costs three
and a natural $A_4$ layer one above twice its degree. This proves
\eqref{eq:rank3-pattern}. In particular a no-$C_3$, no-$A_4$
residual has $d_3(G)\leq2m/9$; neither exclusion can be omitted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Necessary high ternary actions]\label{thm:three-twentieths}
If a transitive normal pair $(A,N)$ of degree $w$ satisfies
$20\delta_A(N)>3w$, its actual action is one of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item natural $C_3$ or natural $A_4$;
\item $6T1,6T4,6T5,6T6$;
\item $12T20,12T85,12T164,12T194,12T228,12T229,12T265$;
\item a transitive $3$-group of degree $9$ or $27$.
\end{enumerate}
This is a necessary list of actions, not a claim that all their normal
pairs have high relative head.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Primitive actions are covered by Lemma~\ref{lem:ternary-block}.
We retain the complete normal-pair catalogues in degrees $6,12,18$:
1,300 actions and 20,410 pairs. Their strict high-action list is
exactly items two and three, with no degree-eighteen hit. In particular
every degree-eighteen pair has head at most two. These remain bounded
classification inputs for the actual actions and all their normal subgroups.

Use a minimal block system and put $v=a_3(R)$ in
\eqref{eq:ternary-block-recurrence}. For a nonexceptional top with
$s\geq3$, $B(s)\leq s/3$. If $r\geq5$, then
\[
 \frac{vs/3+5s/27}{rs}\leq\frac19+\frac5{27r}
 \leq\frac4{27}<\frac3{20};
\]
at $r=4$, $v\leq1$ gives $14s/27<3(4s)/20$.
At $s=2$ the top head vanishes and the stronger primitive density
bound gives $v\leq3r/10$ for $r\geq4$, except possibly $r=9$;
the degree-eighteen input removes that exception.

For a natural $C_3$ top the bound is $v+1$: the only $r\geq4$
case not below $9r/20$ is $r=4$, giving degree twelve. A natural
$A_4$ top gives $v+1\leq3r/5$ for $r\geq4$.
A whole $\mathcal E_9$ top gives
$3v+2\leq27r/20$ for $r\geq6$, and $v\leq1$ deals with $r=4,5$.
For binary blocks $v=0$, only the natural $C_3$ top can fail,
giving degree six.

It remains to treat ternary blocks, $r=3$. For a nonexceptional top,
\[
 B(s)+\lfloor5s/27\rfloor\leq\lfloor9s/20\rfloor
 \qquad(s\notin\{2,6,18\}).
\]
To check all indices, write $s=3^tm$ with $3\nmid m$.
If $m\notin\{1,2\}$, use $1/4+5/27<9/20$.
If $m\in\{1,2\}$ and $t\geq3$, use
$7/27+5/27=4/9<9/20$. For $t\leq2$ the only remaining
indices $s\geq2$ are $2,3,6,9,18$; the sums at $s=3,9$ are
$1,4$ and satisfy the displayed floors. At $s=18$, use the actual
top bound two from the finite input, giving $B(18)+2=8<81/10$.
No separate top-degree $27,54,81,162$ refinement is needed.

The only child degrees still to inspect are $6,9,12,18,27$.
The first, third and fourth use the retained finite catalogues.
Degree nine is covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:rank3-stability}.
At degree 27 a top head at most
one gives $B(9)+1=4\leq81/20$. Otherwise the top is a whole
$\mathcal E_9$ action, hence a $3$-group. Embed the action in
$S_3\wr T$ and put $V=C_3^9$. If the image of the full block kernel
in $C_2^9$ is nontrivial, its coordinate characters on $V$ are all
nontrivial, by top transitivity. Maschke's theorem then makes every
invariant translation subquotient have zero trivial head under
that binary image. The remaining kernel quotient is binary, so
its ternary relative head is zero, and the whole head is at most two.
If that image is trivial, the full block kernel and the top are both
$3$-groups, making the actual degree-27 action a $3$-group. This
exhausts the final case and proves the theorem.
\end{proof}

For the separate bounded counting argument below, we retain the finite
semiregular witnesses for all 91 nonsoluble degree-eighteen actions:
each contains an actual fixed-point-free permutation of order three.
Those witnesses are used for the stronger module-generator capacities
in Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded-s3}; they are not needed for the relative-head
classification just proved.
