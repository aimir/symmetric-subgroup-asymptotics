\section{Exhaustive counting and boundedness-first assembly}
\label{sec:assembly}
Let $F_N$ be the normalized count of all fixed-point-free binary
subgroups of $S_{2N}$, and retain the complete binary error $E_N$
from Section~\ref{sec:markers}. Actual orbit projections give the
exact disjoint partition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-partition}
 F_N=C_{2N}^{\crit}+E_N.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Independent complete binary error]
\label{prop:binary-error}
Both $F_N$ and $E_N$ are bounded, and $E_N=O(2^{-a_BN})$ for
some $a_B>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-recurrence} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:binary-recurrence}
 E_N\leq\eta_B(N)+\sum_{M<N}K_B(N,M)F_M,
 \qquad\eta_B(N)+\sum_{M<N}K_B(N,M)\leq C_B2^{-a_BN}.
\end{equation}
The critical ratios are bounded by Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-intro}.
Choose an onset at which the kernel row is at most $1/2$ and a
constant at least twice the bounded forcing and at least all finitely
many earlier values of $F$. Induction in the exact partition proves
that this constant bounds every $F_N$. Substitution into the same
recurrence gives the asserted exponential error. This induction uses
no ordinary subgroup ratio $A_n=s_n/L_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exhaustive ordinary counting estimate]
\label{thm:global-counting}
There are nonnegative functions $\eta_0,\eta_1,K$ and the exact
marker kernel of \eqref{eq:complete-marker-kernel}, with positive
constants $a_i,C$, such that eventually
\begin{align}\label{eq:global-rates}
 \eta_0(n)&\leq C2^{-a_0n},&
 \eta_1(n)&\leq C2^{-a_1n^2},\notag\\
 \sum_{b<n}K(n,b)&\leq C2^{-a_2n},&
 \sum_{\substack{m<n\\m\ \mathrm{even}}}K_{\rm mark}(n,m)
       &\leq C2^{-a_3n}.
\end{align}
They give the complete inequality
\begin{align}\label{eq:master}
 A_n\leq{}&C_n^{\crit}+\eta_0(n)+\eta_1(n)
             +\sum_{b<n}K(n,b)A_b
             +\mathbf1_{n\ \mathrm{even}}E_{n/2}\notag\\
 &+\sum_{\substack{m<n\\m\ \mathrm{even}}}
              K_{\rm mark}(n,m)E_{m/2}
   +\mathbf1_{n=2N+1}(u_NE_N+v_NE_{N-1}),
\end{align}
where $u_N,v_N$ are the exact coefficients in \eqref{eq:uv}.
There is a single same-degree binary error, and every forward edge
has one complete target.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First take all subgroups with an actual transitive orbit outside
the alphabet consisting of binary groups, natural $C_3,A_4,S_3$,
and fixed points. Theorem~\ref{thm:nonbinary-alphabet} bounds this
complete family by an exponentially small ordinary forward row
and a quadratically small scalar. Every pointing deletes its whole
original orbit, so its exact weight is
$n!/(b!a(U))$. A cold estimate uses it in the form
$\Delta(n,b)/a(U)$ on $A_b$; a hot estimate retains the factor
$1/(b!a(U)p_nG_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor})$ on its complete source sum.
These are bounds for already assembled subgroups, including their
other orbits and any external characters.

In the complementary alphabet let $c,t$ be the numbers of natural
$C_3,A_4$ orbits. If $t\geq n/1000$, apply the complete scalar
of Theorem~\ref{thm:macro-a4}. If $t<n/1000$ and $c+t>0$, apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:low-a4-transfer}. Its intrinsic action condition
holds here: every relative ternary head of a two-group is zero;
the normals $1,C_3,S_3$ of natural $S_3$ also have zero relative
ternary head, since $[C_3,S_3]=C_3$ and $S_3$ has no ternary
abelian quotient. Fixed points contribute zero. This proves the
condition for every residual action in the alphabet, without an
assumption about which normal kernel is selected later. Add the
ordinary row and scalar of this theorem once. The macro and small
packet sectors are disjoint by their original-degree cutoff.

It remains that every moved orbit is binary or natural $S_3$.
Write the marker counts as $g,f$, and $n=2N+\eps$. Under the
exact collapse of Proposition~\ref{prop:complete-marker-collapse},
let $q$ be the number of distinct marker signs and put
$D=(g+f-\eps)/2$. This is an integer, because the binary exterior
has even degree, and is nonnegative. When $D\geq1$, the complete
row in Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-marker-row} applies. If the
collapsed group is critical, its entire contribution is an
exponentially small scalar by Corollary~\ref{cor:pure-marker-sum}.
Otherwise it lies in the complete family defining $E_{m/2}$:
the collapse leaves each pre-existing noncritical binary action
unchanged, and creates only pair actions. Hence this remaining
contribution is precisely bounded by the displayed $K_{\rm mark}$
term. In particular $K_{\rm mark}$ has no unrestricted $A_m$
target.

When $D=0$, in even degree there are no markers or fixed points.
The complete binary partition gives $C_{2N}^{\crit}+E_N$, each
once. In odd degree there is exactly one fixed point or one
natural $S_3$ orbit. With critical binary orbits these are exactly
the two disjoint odd parts of $C_n^{\crit}$. Otherwise their exact
contributions are $\alpha_NE_N$ and $\beta_NpE_N$; the complete
pair moment of Proposition~\ref{prop:binary-moment} bounds their
sum by $u_NE_N+v_NE_{N-1}$. No estimate for a restricted binary
residual is substituted for this complete moment.

These sectors exhaust all actual subgroups and are pairwise
disjoint before their positive enlargements. Within the first
sector fix an order of the proved action families and assign a
subgroup to the first one it meets, before testing temperatures.
For each assigned family its hot part means that some accepted
pointing is hot, and its cold part that none is hot. Each theorem's
complete positive envelope is then paid once, over all its
assigned inputs. Multiple orbit occurrences are already accounted
for by pointing the original weight
$\prod_U(a(U)^{m_U}m_U!)^{-1}$; auxiliary representations and
alternative certificates contribute no new physical factorial.
All subsequent sectors are restrictions of their proved positive
envelopes and are likewise paid once.

Define $\eta_0$ to be the pure-marker scalar, $\eta_1$ the sum of
the nonbinary, macro and small-packet scalars, and $K$ the sum of
the nonbinary and small-packet ordinary rows. Their proved complete
rates and the marker-row theorem give \eqref{eq:global-rates}.
The auxiliary binary kernel $K_B$ is not added to $K$: its role
was to prove the independent estimate for $E$, and the original
even binary count has already been paid with coefficient one.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:binary-error}, $E_N$ is bounded and
decays exponentially.  The elementary envelopes
$u_N\leq4(N+1)$ and $v_N\leq2\Phi^{-2}(N+1)^3$ already suffice:
fixed polynomial factors preserve exponential decay, and their targets
are $N,N-1$.  Hence their contributions in \eqref{eq:master} decay
exponentially. The complete marker row
times the bounded binary errors is exponentially small as well.
The forcing in \eqref{eq:master}, excluding its ordinary row,
is therefore bounded and is $1+O(2^{-cn})$ for some $c>0$.

Choose an onset at which $\sum_{b<n}K(n,b)\leq1/2$ and let
$M$ exceed all finitely many initial $A_b$ and twice a uniform
bound on the forcing. Induction gives $A_n\leq M$ for every $n$.
Substituting this established bound into \eqref{eq:master} now
gives $A_n\leq1+O(2^{-c'n})$. The critical-family lower bound
gives $A_n\geq1-O(2^{-c''n})$. Taking the smaller positive rate
proves the result at both parities. No boundedness premise was
used in deriving any complete row or scalar.
\end{proof}
