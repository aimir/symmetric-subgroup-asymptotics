\section{Reversible simultaneous quotient transport}\label{sec:transport}
The construction below permits nonabelian common quotients and proper
subdirect replacement groups. These are actual group maps, rather than
identifications of abstract quotient types.

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous reconstruction]\label{prop:transport}
Let $H\leq\prod_iU_i$ be full subdirect. For selected coordinates let
$N_i=H\cap U_i$ be the exact embedded coordinate axis, and specify maps
\[
 \alpha_i:U_i\twoheadrightarrow Q_i=U_i/N_i,\qquad
 \beta_i:P_i\twoheadrightarrow Q_i,
 \qquad P_i\leq\prod_{V\in W_i}V
\]
with $P_i$ full on the displayed word $W_i$. Use identity maps on the
untouched coordinates. Set $\alpha=\prod_i\alpha_i$,
$\beta=\prod_i\beta_i$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport}
 \overline H=\alpha(H),\qquad H_{\rm new}=\beta^{-1}(\overline H).
\end{equation}
Then $H_{\rm new}$ is full on every replacement factor. The decorated
map is injective, with inverse
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transport-inverse}
 H=\alpha^{-1}(\beta(H_{\rm new})).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The embedded $N_i$ commute and each is contained in $H$. Hence
$\ker\alpha=\prod_iN_i\leq H$, proving $H=\alpha^{-1}(\overline H)$.
The projection of $\overline H$ onto each $Q_i$ is full. Any element
of $P_i$ has an image appearing in some element of $\overline H$;
surjectivity of the other $\beta_j$ permits a simultaneous lift. Thus
the projection of $H_{\rm new}$ onto the whole $P_i$ is full, which
implies fullness on its displayed factors. Surjectivity of $\beta$
gives $\beta(H_{\rm new})=\overline H$, proving the inverse formula.
No step replaces $P_i$ by the full product of its projections.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded decoration fibre]\label{prop:decoration}
Suppose the original actions, words, and maps range over fixed finite
menus of bounded degree, each replacement has the same physical degree
as its original coordinate, and original critical coordinates are
untouched. If the original noncritical support has degree $b$, the
number of decorated preimages of any target subgroup on $n$ points is
at most $(n+2)^{K b}$ for a menu-dependent constant $K$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Realize each replacement on the same labelled points as its original
orbit. To reconstruct the decorated block structure, select and group
the target orbits into the changed bounded blocks, and record the
finitely many local action types, coordinate bijections, normal axes,
and map choices. There are $O(b)$ selected orbits, grouping slots, and
finite labels. Allowing $n+2$ choices at each slot bounds all these
possibilities by $(n+2)^{K b}$. For fixed decorations the original
subgroup is unique by \eqref{eq:transport-inverse}.
This counts labelled groups directly, so division by $n!$ retains
the original action-normalizer weights and occurrence factorials.
\end{proof}

The original denominator can also be retained directly when only an
upper bound is needed; uniqueness of the orbit presentation is unnecessary.

\begin{lemma}[Upper assembly with original normalizers]\label{lem:upper-assembly}
Fix nonempty finite sets $\Omega_i$, actions $U_i\leq S(\Omega_i)$,
and multiplicities $m_i$, and let $X$ be the disjoint union of these
occurrences, of total size $n$. Put
\[
 W=\prod_i\bigl(N_{S(\Omega_i)}(U_i)\wr S_{m_i}\bigr)\leq S(X).
\]
If $\mathcal P$ is any family of subgroups of $S(X)$ invariant under
conjugation by $W$, then its assembled family
$\mathcal A=\{gKg^{-1}:g\in S(X),\ K\in\mathcal P\}$ satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal A|\leq
 \frac{n!\,|\mathcal P|}
 {\prod_i |N_{S(\Omega_i)}(U_i)|^{m_i}m_i!}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every pair $(g,K)$ and every $w\in W$, the pair
$(gw,w^{-1}Kw)$ has the same assembled subgroup. It remains in
$S(X)\times\mathcal P$ by invariance, and distinct $w$ give distinct
first coordinates. Every nonempty assembly fibre therefore has at
least $|W|$ elements. Summing the fibre sizes gives
$|\mathcal A||W|\leq n!|\mathcal P|$. The displayed denominator is
the order of the original internal-symmetry group $W$.
\end{proof}

No transitivity, separation of the action types, or fullness of the
local subgroups is required for this upper bound. Invariance is a
condition on the whole family; individual subgroups need not be fixed.

Here is the quantitative form needed to use such a transport. Suppose
a target binary word has critical rank $R$, $a$ regular $C_4$ factors,
and noncritical carrier support of half-degree $4T$, so
$N=R+2a+4T$. Put $C_{\rm new}=2a+4T$ and write the original
noncritical support as $2C_{\rm old}$. Assume each replacement retains
at least a fixed proportion $\delta>0$ of noncritical points. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-transport}
 \delta C_{\rm old}\leq C_{\rm new}\leq C_{\rm old}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Absorbing decorations]\label{prop:absorb-decorations}
Assume the complete normalized target mass at each profile satisfies
the two uniform estimates
\begin{align}\label{eq:mixture-envelopes}
 T\leq a/140,\quad a\geq1:\quad&
 2^{-aN/25+O(a\log(N+2))},\notag\\
 T>a/140:\quad&
 2^{-(29/656)TN+O(N\log(N+2))}.
\end{align}
With the finite-menu assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:decoration}
and \eqref{eq:support-transport}, the full original family with a
noncritical orbit has normalized mass $2^{-\Omega(N)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First remove the original range $0<C_{\rm old}\leq\sqrt N/4$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:small-support}, before charging any decorations.
Indeed its noncritical degree is at most $\sqrt N/2$, and its critical
degree is at least $N$ for large $N$. The remaining range satisfies
$C_{\rm new}>\delta\sqrt N/4$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:decoration}, after summing over original supports
the additional factor is $2^{O_\delta(C_{\rm new}\log(N+2))}$.

If $T\leq a/140$, then $C_{\rm new}<3a$. This factor is absorbed by
the first error in \eqref{eq:mixture-envelopes}, and the negative term
is at most $-N/25$ before the smaller error. If $T>a/140$, then
$C_{\rm new}<284T$, so $T>\delta\sqrt N/1136$. The negative term
in the second envelope has order at least $N^{3/2}$ and absorbs both
the displayed error and the decoration cost. There are only polynomially
many profiles and support sizes. Their sum retains the exponential gain.
\end{proof}

The inequalities \eqref{eq:mixture-envelopes} are the stronger profile
bounds proved for the full twelve-carrier mixture in
Theorem~\ref{thm:binary-mixture}.
Multiplying only a final $2^{-\gamma N}$ mixture bound by the decoration
factor would be insufficient. An all-critical replacement also fails
\eqref{eq:support-transport}, and is outside the negligible-mass
conclusion even though the reconstruction map remains valid.
