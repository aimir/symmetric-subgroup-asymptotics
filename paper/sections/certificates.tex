\section{Finite evidence and reproduction}\label{sec:certificates}
The accompanying computations distinguish three assertions: a literal
record satisfies its algebraic equations; a stated finite domain is
covered by such records; and an infinite counting theorem follows from
that finite domain together with proved structural and summation
lemmas. These assertions require different arguments.

The publication package contains the following mathematical data.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.29\linewidth}p{0.61\linewidth}}
\toprule
Dataset & Mathematical role\\
\midrule
Small binary actions & Literal actions through degree $16$, normal
axes, quotient maps, and the four local witness types.\\
Carrier data & Twelve noncritical actions, normal profiles, transition
data, and specified quotient-replacement maps.\\
Nonbinary and rank data & Finite action and normal-rank domains used
in the selected primitive and pair-top reductions.\\
Pair-top data & Concrete modules and constructive witnesses in the
count-twelve and count-sixteen domains.\\
Four- and six-block data & Constructive classifications and actual
quotient interfaces.\\
Zero-ternary frame data & Thirty complete binary module lattices and
thirty-eight literal odd-subgroup/normalizer witnesses.\\
Critical and audit data & Quadratic realization constants, critical
normalizers, and independent local chart controls.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The data contracts specify literal point actions, normal subgroups,
source and target maps, and the original symmetric normalizers. A
quotient order or isomorphism-type name alone does not specify a
counting witness. For a proper subdirect replacement, the image group
and both its projection maps are part of the record.

For the critical quadratic constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic},
one can enumerate $\GL(2,2)$ and $\GL(4,2)$ and test the equation
$q(gv)=q(v)$ on every vector. This establishes the orders $2$ and $72$
for the displayed forms. The torsor argument in the lemma is what
extends these finite calculations to arbitrary source dimension.
Likewise the exact rational inequality \eqref{eq:positive-dual} is a
finite arithmetic certificate whose implication for all degree-$32$
sections is proved by the preceding incidence argument.

The classification libraries are pinned to
\textsf{TransGrp}~3.6.5 and \textsf{PrimGrp}~3.4.4
\cite{TransGrp365,PrimGrp344}; their mathematical construction and
scope are described in \cite{Hulpke2005,HoltRoyle2020,RoneyDougal2005Primitive}.
The recorded interpreter is GAP~4.13.1~\cite{GAP4131}.
Where a coverage proof uses an independent constructive generation
argument, the catalogue is a source of literal representatives, not
its sole completeness argument.

All reproduction commands are local. The file
\texttt{computations/README.md} documents the producers and independent
checkers, and \texttt{certificates/manifests/finite\_witnesses.json}
binds their finite input and output files. Hashes identify bytes; they
do not prove a mathematical theorem. The checks do not initialize or
invoke a proof assistant.

The same-source $C_3$ example, the inverse-complement reciprocal, the
proper-subdirect transport examples, and the binary duplicate factor
$1/4$ are regression conditions on the counting maps. Passing these
conditions alone does not establish an asymptotic theorem. The binary
coverage principles are proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-coverage}; the
universal mixture and recurrence arguments are proved in
Theorems~\ref{thm:binary-mixture} and~\ref{thm:binary-recurrence}.
The complete nonbinary exhaustion is proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonbinary-alphabet}; the ordinary counting inequality
and its boundedness-first induction are proved in Section~\ref{sec:assembly}.
The supplementary affine-nine and narrow socket data provide independent
local checks; the proof above uses the general frame and mixture arguments
for those domains.


\subsection{Formal verification}
The accompanying Lean development fixes Lean~4.30.0 and a precise mathlib
revision. Its final theorem proves Theorems~\ref{thm:main},
\ref{thm:saddle} and~\ref{thm:elementary} relative to seven explicit
published mathematical inputs. Their complete formulations, Lean types
and publication locators appear in \texttt{formal/README.md}. No finite
primitive or transitive catalogue correspondence is a parameter of the
final theorem. The project-specific source, capacity, marked-moment and
summation statements are proved internally. Both final exports have only
the standard logical axioms of Lean in addition to their stated parameters.

An optional development starts from a pinned formulation of CFSG and
proves model-specific inputs on its original concrete groups. In
particular, for $q=p^f$ it establishes
\[
 |\operatorname{Out}(F_4(q))|\leq2f,\qquad
 |\operatorname{Out}({}^3D_4(q))|\leq3f,\qquad
 |\operatorname{Out}({}^2E_6(q))|\leq6f.
\]
The proofs include every characteristic, retain the actual signed root
charts and inner scalar tori, and do not assume that arbitrary abstract
automorphisms lift to the matrix source. Together with proved index and
Sylow bounds, they remove these families from the optional reduction.
For the original simple $G_2(q)$ model with $q\geq3$, an actual
singular-ray action gives $\ell<3q^5$, while two disjoint Sylow images
give $q^{12}\leq|G_2(q)|$. Thus its least proper subgroup index also
satisfies $\ell^2<|G_2(q)|$, including $q=3$. Its outer-order budget
remains an explicit obligation of the optional reduction.
That reduction still has explicit residual model hypotheses and five
published counting or generation inputs; it is not a proof from CFSG
alone. The seven-input theorem stated above remains the canonical
formalization of the asymptotic results.

The coarse all-subgroup estimate is derived internally from the retained
soluble-generation input alone. For soluble primitive actions, the
composition and subgroup-rank bounds used in its large-orbit step follow
unconditionally from the actual affine structure. For every fixed orbit cutoff $C$, the actual
bounded nilpotent family has size at most
\[
  2^{b^2/16+A_C b\log_2(b+2)}.
\]
Its proof retains the binary and odd nilpotent sectors and their joint
subdirect words. An actual soluble container menu of size
$2^{O(b\log^4(b+2))}$, together with original-support compression and the
large-orbit generator bound, gives
$s_n\leq 2^{n^2/16+\beta n\sqrt n}$. This coarse estimate is therefore
not an additional parameter of the final formal theorem.

The same constructed soluble cover pays the marked large-orbit encoding:
one containing group is recorded together with the original relative
tuple and its character restrictions. Its subquadratic menu cost is
absorbed uniformly in the marking parameter. Consequently the global
marked-moment branch needs no labelled-transitive-count premise; that
published input is retained only for the separate nonsoluble pre-E$_7$
action menu.

Several classical results are proved internally: Burnside's two-prime
solvability theorem; semisimplicity and conjugacy of Clifford constituents
over an arbitrary field; the binary normal-subgroup bound
\[
 \#\{N\mathrel{\unlhd}G\}\,2^{-\ell^2/4}\leq 8\,2^{2\ell}
 \qquad (|G|=2^\ell);
\]
simplicity of $\operatorname{PSL}_3(F)$; and
$\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_5)\cong S_5$.
The projective proofs use elementary matrices and faithful projective
actions, without ATLAS code. The selected proof ports preserve their
Apache-2.0 attribution and exact revisions in the formal sources.
Full abelianization-order bounds for faithful permutation actions,
including higher prime-power cyclic factors, supply both abelian-quotient
fields of the character interface. The faithful-character tuple theorem,
nilpotent class-number bound and arbitrary-normal-target bounded-factor
epimorphism theorem are also proved internally.

The refined induced-module theorem keeps one uniform generator capacity
satisfying the half-dimension, logarithmic, coprime, primary and prime-power
bounds simultaneously. Its proof uses actual Sylow--Mackey pieces,
finite chain widths, Nakayama's lemma and coprime semisimplicity. The two
soluble-orbit consumers are proved on the actual permutation module and
on the actual globally normal ternary core. These replacements do not
assert the stronger unused arbitrary-local per-orbit statement.
Raw O'Nan--Scott structure is proved using actual minimal normal subgroups,
the full socle, point-stabilizer maximality and the exact inner kernel in
the outer-wreath action. The quantitative simple-index inequalities remain
an explicit published input. They also imply $|\operatorname{Aut}(S)|\leq|S|^2$
for every finite nonabelian simple $S$, so simple two-generation is not a
separate input. The primitive relative ternary-head tail follows directly
from actual affine orders and full-socle quotient orders, as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:ternary-block}; no ordinary primitive normal-generation
theorem is required.

Exact binary excess classification is now proved internally, including
all degree and proper-normal restrictions. The proof retains the original
pair kernels, joint parity relations and square carries, and checks the
finite scalar certificates in Lean's kernel. It also proves the full
original abelianization consequences. Together with the intrinsic
proper-normal bound, it supplies the three original dependent-word prices
without changing their factors or maximal fully projecting tail.
Roney-Dougal--Tracey's full binary normal-$F$ quotient theorem is also
proved internally. Its commutator is the ambient $[F,Q]$; the centralizer,
original quotient, exact excess alternatives and excess-one generator
equality are all retained, including the degree-eight exception.

The last degree-$32$ excess-one configurations have kernel head and top
rank $(4,5)$ or $(3,6)$. In the first, actual joint layer bounds give
$|G'|\leq1024$ and top order at most $1024$; every nonabelian original
quotient has centre at most $256$. The small-denominator case uses the
same original second centre: its constant-displacement kernel axis has
order at most $16$, and its image in the actual top centre has order at
most $16$. This joint bound avoids assuming faithfulness at a
half-dimension boundary.

The second configuration has four actual kernel models. Their original
normal quotients use joint source bounds or a proper normal subgroup
constructed from an invariant interval $A<S$ in the actual pair module.
The full lower layer and the same normal denominator are retained.
A selected central constant flip has zero class in the original affine
cocycle, proved by its actual commutator and annihilator identities;
neither extension is assumed split. The mixed commutator and centralizer
are then bounded together through that interval.

The current primitive consumers use actual linear and full-socle structure
rather than bounded catalogue correspondence. This proves the ternary
chief-weight and $3/10$ budgets and the ordinary Dixon order bound.
The strict-head bound is used away from degrees $10$ and $12$; the complete
physical $C_3$ source pays those two actual semisimple sectors separately.
This route does not claim the stronger unused global strict-head theorem.
The actual degree-eighteen bound and sufficient semisimple compression
margin $3q\leq2r$ complete the primitive consumers.

Small affine sources keep their actual block systems, translation kernels,
normal axes and quotient fibres. Degree eight has complete checked linear
and transitive action coverage. Degree sixteen uses the proved
$\operatorname{GL}_4(\mathbf F_2)\cong A_8$ geometry and the original
translation kernel; degree twenty-seven uses proved linear structure.
The degree-five affine classification and degree-$25$ projective facts are internal. Degree $81$ uses its complete linear-order budget; degrees $32$ and $64$ use original Clifford constituents, scalar fields and simple normal subgroups. The general affine component bound uses actual chief charges and full affine-order divisors. No primitive composition-length theorem is a parameter of the final exports. Complete
original twelve-pair and sixteen-pair sources therefore require no action
catalogue premise. Stronger optional classifiers and their finite witness
packages remain separate from the final proof route.

Finally, the marked moment uses the now-derived coarse subgroup bound and the constructed soluble-container menu. Its soluble nilpotent core has the proved
ordinary supplement bound $L(B!+1)\lfloor\sqrt M\rfloor$ on the original
bounded-orbit source, with $L=B=C!$ and $M=2b$. This changes only a fixed
constant and does not claim the sharper unrestricted $4L\sqrt M$ theorem.
The original cutoff, action weights, character restrictions and terminal
attachment are preserved. Recursive local source/object verification and
exported-declaration axiom audits accompany the kernel checks; detailed
build records are kept outside the publication repository.
