\section{Finite evidence and reproduction}\label{sec:certificates}
The accompanying computations distinguish three assertions: a literal
record satisfies its algebraic equations; a stated finite domain is
covered by such records; and an infinite counting theorem follows from
that finite domain together with proved structural and summation
lemmas. These assertions require different arguments.

The publication package contains the following mathematical data.

\midrule

Carrier data & Twelve noncritical actions, normal profiles, transition
data, and specified quotient-replacement maps.\\

in the selected primitive and pair-top reductions.\\

\bottomrule

\end{center}
The data contracts specify literal point actions, normal subgroups,
source and target maps, and the original symmetric normalizers. A
quotient order or isomorphism-type name alone does not specify a
counting witness. For a proper subdirect replacement, the image group
and both its projection maps are part of the record.

For the critical quadratic constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic},
one can enumerate $\GL(2,2)$ and $\GL(4,2)$ and test the equation
$q(gv)=q(v)$ on every vector. This establishes the orders $2$ and $72$
for the displayed forms. The torsor argument in the lemma is what
extends these finite calculations to arbitrary source dimension.
Likewise the exact rational inequality \eqref{eq:positive-dual} is a
finite arithmetic certificate whose implication for all degree-$32$
sections is proved by the preceding incidence argument.

The classification libraries are pinned to
\textsf{TransGrp}~3.6.5 and \textsf{PrimGrp}~3.4.4
\cite{TransGrp365,PrimGrp344}; their mathematical construction and
scope are described in \cite{Hulpke2005,HoltRoyle2020,RoneyDougal2005Primitive}.
The recorded interpreter is GAP~4.13.1~\cite{GAP4131}.
Where a coverage proof uses an independent constructive generation
argument, the catalogue is a source of literal representatives, not
its sole completeness argument.

All reproduction commands are local. The file
\texttt{computations/README.md} documents the producers and independent
checkers, and \texttt{certificates/manifests/finite\_witnesses.json}
binds their finite input and output files. Hashes identify bytes; they
do not prove a mathematical theorem. The checks do not initialize or
invoke a proof assistant.

The same-source $C_3$ example, the inverse-complement reciprocal, the
proper-subdirect transport examples, and the binary duplicate factor
$1/4$ are regression conditions on the counting maps. Passing these
conditions alone does not establish an asymptotic theorem. The binary
coverage principles are proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-coverage}; the
universal mixture and recurrence arguments are proved in
Theorems~\ref{thm:binary-mixture} and~\ref{thm:binary-recurrence}.
The complete nonbinary exhaustion is proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonbinary-alphabet}; the ordinary counting inequality
and its boundedness-first induction are proved in Section~\ref{sec:assembly}.
The supplementary affine-nine and narrow socket data provide independent
local checks; the proof above uses the general frame and mixture arguments
for those domains.


\subsection{Formal verification}
The accompanying Lean development fixes Lean~4.30.0 and a precise mathlib
revision. Its final theorem proves Theorems~\ref{thm:main},
\ref{thm:saddle} and~\ref{thm:elementary} relative to 27 explicit published
mathematical and catalogue inputs, whose formulations and source locators
are listed in \texttt{formal/README.md}. The project-specific source,
capacity, marked-moment and summation statements are proved internally.
The exported theorems use only the standard logical axioms of Lean in
addition to their stated parameters.

Four formerly external facts are also proved in the formal development:
Burnside's two-prime solvability theorem; semisimplicity and conjugacy of
constituents in Clifford restriction over an arbitrary field; the bound
\[
 \#\{N\mathrel{\unlhd}G\}\,2^{-\ell^2/4}\leq 8\,2^{2\ell}
 \qquad (|G|=2^\ell);
\]
and simplicity of $\operatorname{PSL}_3(F)$ for every field $F$, including
$\mathbf F_2$ and $\mathbf F_3$. The subgroup estimate follows from a
stronger checked subgroup-interval theorem. The projective simplicity
proof uses elementary-matrix generation, perfectness and Iwasawa's
criterion; it uses no ATLAS code. These four facts are supplied internally
and are not among the 27 remaining inputs. Ported proofs retain their
Apache-2.0 attribution and exact source revisions in the formal sources.


The exceptional isomorphism $\operatorname{PGL}_2(\mathbf F_5)\cong S_5$
and the complete degree-five affine classification are also proved
internally. The former uses the six-point projective action and an
order-$120$ subgroup recognition argument in $S_6$. The latter constructs
actual translation coordinates and retains point conjugacy for the natural
$F_{20}$ action. Separately, the half-dimension and logarithmic induced-module
bounds are derived from the retained refined Tracey input, and the
faithful-simple logarithmic generator bound from the retained simple-group
two-generation input. These three implications remove redundant parameters;
the stronger two published theorems remain among the 27 explicit inputs.


The separate primitive degree-six and degree-nine inputs follow from the
retained bounded composition catalogue. In degree six, actual coset actions
and the degree-twelve order list supply the subgroup recognition step.
Likewise the binary permutation formula follows from the refined Tracey
input, and the soluble permutation formula from the retained soluble-orbit
induced-module theorem. The formal proof constructs the actual stabilizer
induction and an odd Hall subgroup, the latter using the checked
Schur--Zassenhaus theorem. An explicit integer cap preserves uniformity over
all subrepresentations. These are dependency reductions; the broader
catalogue and induced-module theorems remain explicit assumptions. The
repeated standalone abelianization parameter is supplied internally by a
checked theorem.


The soluble subdirect socle-length statement is proved internally using
integral conjugation modules and the exact joint socle intersection,
followed by induction on the actual socle quotients. Both degree-six block
classification inputs are also proved from faithful wreath coordinates,
without a finite action catalogue. The three-pair proof uses
$C_2\wr S_3\cong S_4\times C_2$; the two-triple proof retains the actual
axes, irreducible quarter-turn and full-wreath charts. Finally the ordinary
Dixon order bound is derived from the retained primitive catalogue,
O'Nan--Scott structure and soluble irreducible linear cube bound, followed
by actual block and orbit induction. These three broader inputs remain
explicit; the standalone Dixon parameter is removed.


The full-order bound $|A|\leq p^{\lfloor n/p\rfloor}$ for abelian
$p$-quotients of faithful degree-$n$ actions is now proved internally,
by central-prime block and orbit induction followed by actual Sylow
restriction. Higher prime-power factors are retained, so the binary field
of the character-literature interface is no longer an assumption. The
mixed-prime abelianization bound is also proved internally: a minimal
subgroup surjecting onto the actual abelian quotient is nilpotent, and its
coprime factors admit faithful actions on disjoint original supports.
The faithful-character tuple theorem is proved from the actual socle normal
generators, primary semisimple duality, coinduction and irreducible
constituent selection, including the empty tuple. The bounded-factor
epimorphism theorem is proved with an arbitrary normal target subgroup:
short original generating tuples retain their literal product support,
and joint kernel incidence pays the centralizer and commutator factors
together. The nilpotent class-number bound is also proved: binary and ternary
actions satisfy fourth-power estimates by a commuting-pair count across a
prime-index block kernel, and large primes use the faithful-action order
bound. Actual disjoint coprime supports complete the nilpotent argument.
These four results are no longer assumptions. The binary degree-eight
excess-two implication is also proved internally: complete checked coverage
of the 26 actions and short normal-generation words exclude the other
25 actions, while the surviving $E_8$ has equal center and derived subgroup
of order two. Its nonabelian normal quotients have trivial denominator.

Lemma~\ref{lem:soluble-orbit-generation} is formalized using the retained
primitive normal-generator, composition-length and refined Tracey inputs.
The proof keeps the correlated actions on all original orbits in its
quotient and uses ordinary generation through actual abelian chief kernels.
It supplies the large-orbit cutoff internally. Finally the container menu
uses the retained labelled transitive-group count at the weaker
subquadratic scale established above. The sharper Pyber representative
bound is unnecessary for the final marked moment; no formal proof of that
stronger class-count theorem is claimed.

The degree-eight primitive-affine complement classification is proved from
complete subgroup coverage of the actual $\operatorname{GL}_3(\mathbf F_2)$.
Single-element adjunction certificates cover all fifteen subgroup classes;
fixed nonzero vector or covector flags exclude twelve, and literal linear
intertwiners identify the three remaining canonical complements. The
small-affine catalogue parameter now retains only degrees sixteen and
twenty-seven. The full transitive degree-eight catalogue is a separate
remaining input.
