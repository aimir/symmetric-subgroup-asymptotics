\section{Finite evidence and reproduction}\label{sec:certificates}
The accompanying computations distinguish three assertions: a literal
record satisfies its algebraic equations; a stated finite domain is
covered by such records; and an infinite counting theorem follows from
that finite domain together with proved structural and summation
lemmas. These assertions require different arguments.

The publication package contains the following mathematical data.

The data contracts specify literal point actions, normal subgroups,
source and target maps, and the original symmetric normalizers. A
quotient order or isomorphism-type name alone does not specify a
counting witness. For a proper subdirect replacement, the image group
and both its projection maps are part of the record.

For the critical quadratic constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:quadratic},
one can enumerate $\GL(2,2)$ and $\GL(4,2)$ and test the equation
$q(gv)=q(v)$ on every vector. This establishes the orders $2$ and $72$
for the displayed forms. The torsor argument in the lemma is what
extends these finite calculations to arbitrary source dimension.
Likewise the exact rational inequality \eqref{eq:positive-dual} is a
finite arithmetic certificate whose implication for all degree-$32$
sections is proved by the preceding incidence argument.

The classification libraries are pinned to
\textsf{TransGrp}~3.6.5 and \textsf{PrimGrp}~3.4.4
\cite{TransGrp365,PrimGrp344}; their mathematical construction and
scope are described in \cite{Hulpke2005,HoltRoyle2020,RoneyDougal2005Primitive}.
The recorded interpreter is GAP~4.13.1~\cite{GAP4131}.
Where a coverage proof uses an independent constructive generation
argument, the catalogue is a source of literal representatives, not
its sole completeness argument.

All reproduction commands are local. The file
\texttt{computations/README.md} documents the producers and independent
checkers, and \texttt{certificates/manifests/finite\_witnesses.json}
binds their finite input and output files. Hashes identify bytes; they
do not prove a mathematical theorem. The checks do not initialize or
invoke a proof assistant.

The same-source $C_3$ example, the inverse-complement reciprocal, the
proper-subdirect transport examples, and the binary duplicate factor
$1/4$ are regression conditions on the counting maps. Passing these
conditions alone does not establish an asymptotic theorem. The binary
coverage principles are proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:binary-coverage}; the
universal mixture and recurrence arguments are proved in
Theorems~\ref{thm:binary-mixture} and~\ref{thm:binary-recurrence}.
The complete nonbinary exhaustion is proved in
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonbinary-alphabet}; the ordinary counting inequality
and its boundedness-first induction are proved in Section~\ref{sec:assembly}.
The supplementary affine-nine and narrow socket data provide independent
local checks; the proof above uses the general frame and mixture arguments
for those domains.


\subsection{Formal verification}
The accompanying Lean development fixes Lean~4.30.0 and a precise mathlib
revision. Its final theorem proves Theorems~\ref{thm:main},
\ref{thm:saddle} and~\ref{thm:elementary} relative to seven explicit
published mathematical inputs. Their complete formulations, Lean types
and publication locators appear in \texttt{formal/README.md}. No finite
primitive or transitive catalogue correspondence is a parameter of the
final theorem. The project-specific source, capacity, marked-moment and
summation statements are proved internally. Both final exports have only
the standard logical axioms of Lean in addition to their stated parameters.

An optional development starts from a pinned formulation of CFSG and
proves model-specific inputs on its original concrete groups. In
particular, for $q=p^f$ it establishes
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |\operatorname{Out}(G_2(q))|&\leq2f,&
 |\operatorname{Out}(F_4(q))|&\leq2f,\\
 |\operatorname{Out}({}^3D_4(q))|&\leq3f,&
 |\operatorname{Out}({}^2E_6(q))|&\leq6f.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The proofs include every characteristic, retain the actual signed root
charts and inner scalar tori, and do not assume that arbitrary abstract
automorphisms lift to the matrix source. Together with proved index and
Sylow bounds, they remove these families from the optional reduction.
For the original simple $G_2(q)$ model with $q\geq3$, an actual
singular-ray action gives $\ell<3q^5$, while two disjoint Sylow images
give $q^{12}\leq|G_2(q)|$. Thus its least proper subgroup index also
satisfies $\ell^2<|G_2(q)|$, including $q=3$. The original torus gives
$q\leq\ell$; together with the proved outer bound this discharges its
full outer-order budget. Both residual model hypotheses now exclude
$G_2$. For the original Ree group ${}^2G_2(q)$, the literal fixed group
has a proved two-cell Bruhat decomposition, order
$q^3(q-1)(q^3+1)$, and a soluble positive Sylow normalizer. Its two
opposite positive subgroups have nontrivial normal image in the actual
simple derived central quotient, giving the required generating Sylow
witnesses there. Thus ${}^2G_2$ is also removed from the good-Sylow
hypothesis. The actual Borel point action gives $\ell\leq q^3+1$.
The full derived centre is trivial and the fixed-to-derived index is at
most four, so
$4|{}^2G_2(q)|\geq q^3(q-1)(q^3+1)$. These bounds also prove
$\ell^2<|{}^2G_2(q)|$ for $q\geq27$. The actual central root line
and square torus give $q\leq\ell$. The opposed Sylow pair's stabilizer
restricts faithfully to the positive group; its characteristic layers
and cubing map give central-axis rigidity. Counting this central action
and charging the same inner derived torus proves
$|\operatorname{Out}({}^2G_2(3^{2m+1}))|\leq2(2m+1)$.
This discharges the outer-order budget as well, so both residual model
hypotheses exclude ${}^2G_2$. The other Ree family, the Tits group and
sporadic groups retain their full payloads.
That reduction still has explicit residual model hypotheses and five
published counting or generation inputs; it is not a proof from CFSG
alone. The seven-input theorem stated above remains the canonical
formalization of the asymptotic results.

The auxiliary class-count reductions include a shared basis-stabilizer
argument: a finite faithful linear group preserving a nondegenerate
bilinear form over a field of order $q$ has order at most
$q^{n(n+1)/2}$ in dimension $n$. The same counting argument applies
to separating sesquilinear pairings. It sharpens the classical-model
order bounds while retaining the original socle and degree witnesses.
A separate trace and centralizer argument proves $k(\mathrm{SL}_2(q))\leq q+4$
over every finite field. A central-involution orbit count gives
$2k(\mathrm{PSL}_2(q))\leq q+5$ in odd characteristic. Together with
actual torus and root actions and the small alternating cases, this
closes the original rank-one component class budget over every permitted
finite field, for both original degree forms. At field order nine, an
order-360 index-gap argument either identifies the model with $A_6$ from
an index-six subgroup or supplies the stronger proper-index lower bound
eight. No finite subgroup census is assumed in this argument.
Literal three-dimensional normal forms give the all-field bound
$k(\mathrm{SL}_3(q))\leq\gcd(q-1,3)(q^2+2q)$. The corresponding
scalar-centralizer class-fibre bound and the sharper outer-order budget
close the original rank-two component capacity as well. Over the
two-element field, the proved order 168 and a faithful coset action force
every proper index to be at least seven.
The class equation also gives $k(S)\leq |S|/b+1$ for a center-free
group whose proper indices are at least $b$; exact matrix orders and
central quotients close further type-$A$ parameters with this shared
test. The same class-equation capacity test closes seven further
orthogonal, twisted-orthogonal and unitary parameter cases, retaining
the proved outer-order factor $8f$ in the twisted-orthogonal application. For the original
odd-characteristic $C_2$ model, a coupled
Heisenberg--$\mathrm{SL}_2$ stabilizer argument proves proper index at
least $q^2$. One numerical induction then pays its component order cube
for every $q\geq7$.
Over a prime field the coupled argument improves the index bound to
$q^3$, which pays the two small odd fields as well. The whole
odd-characteristic $C_2$ branch is therefore closed.
In every higher rank $r\geq3$, the original odd-characteristic
symplectic model has proper index at least $p q^{2r-2}$, by the coupled
Heisenberg kernel and full symplectic tail stabilizer. The proof retains
genuine prime-field annihilators and an explicit transvection-word
embedding. A shared numerical template pays the component cube, closing
the entire odd-characteristic symplectic class-count branch.
The two binary type-$A$ parameters left by these order tests are closed
by one cyclic-polynomial template. Equal multisets of actual cyclic
factors give intertwining linear equivalences; the checked interior-bit
covers have 18 codes in dimension four and 104 in dimension six.
Surjective matrix and central-quotient maps transfer these bounds to
the original projective models, without exceptional isomorphisms or
enumeration of their matrices.
In characteristic two, the actual symmetric shear subgroup and its
general-linear normalizer give the $q^2$ proper-index bound for $q\geq8$
as well. This argument retains the genuine prime-field annihilator of
the actual stabilizer kernel, without asserting extension-field
linearity. The same component budget therefore closes these $C_2$
capacities; field order four remains open. A sharper projective-level
argument proves $q(q-1)^2\leq2[S:H]$ in characteristic two, giving index
at least 18 at field order four, but this does not yet pay that case's
class-count capacity.
In higher rank $r\geq3$, the characteristic-two argument now proves
\[
  (q-1)q^{r-2}(q^r-q)\leq 2[S:H]
\]
for every proper subgroup of the same original model. A genuine
prime-field functional reconstructs a quadratic form, whose two-frame
orbit is counted with the same actual kernel quotient and projected
stabilizer. The bound pays the component capacity when $q\geq4$ or
$r\geq5$; at field order two it retains
$2^{r-3}(2^r-2)\leq[S:H]$.
The actual symplectic realization transfers the same result to
characteristic-two type-$B$ models, inheriting simplicity from that
original model. For split orthogonal type $D$, actual elementary words
are transitive on every nonzero quadratic level. The common level-slice
count and the same genuine prime-field annihilator give
$(q^{r-1}-1)q^{r-2}\leq[S:H]$, with no extra prime multiplier.
This pays every field order at least three and every rank at least five,
leaving only $D_4(2)$ in this family.
In odd characteristic the actual type-$B$ point radical gives
$(q^{r-1}-1)q^{r-1}\leq[S:H]$ in every rank $r\geq3$.
This pays all such original component capacities, leaving only
$B_3(2)$ and $B_4(2)$ in type $B$.
For twisted orthogonal type $D$, the actual point radical, including its
rank-three anisotropic-tail base, gives

prime-field annihilator and the tail stabilizer belong to the same subgroup;
there is no additional prime multiplier. This pays all field orders at

For unitary rank two, the actual nonabelian radical's central intersection,
vector image and projected torus subgroup are retained from the same
