\section{The small natural alternating packet}
\label{sec:packet-transfer}
Let $\mathcal U$ be the class of allowed transitive actions consisting
of natural $C_3,A_4$ and actions $U$ for which every literal normal
subgroup $N$ satisfies $\delta_U(N)\leq3\deg(U)/20$.
This is an explicit intrinsic action predicate. The following theorem
does not assert that an arbitrary residual already lies in $\mathcal U$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete low-packet transfer]\label{thm:low-a4-transfer}
For subgroups all of whose actions lie in $\mathcal U$, the sector
with fewer than $n/1000$ natural $A_4$ orbits and at least one
natural $C_3$ or $A_4$ orbit has a complete ordinary forward row
$O(2^{-n/500})$ and scalar at most $2^{-n^2/80000}$ eventually.
The complementary sector with at least $n/1000$ natural $A_4$
orbits has the scalar in Theorem~\ref{thm:macro-a4}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An actual-orbit filtration gives, for a group $K$ with no natural
$C_3$ orbit and $t$ natural $A_4$ orbits,
$d_3(K)\leq3\deg(K)/20+2t/5$.
First delete all $c\geq1$ natural $C_3$ orbits. The remaining
degree is $b=n-3c$ and the original-degree cutoff gives
\[
 d_3(K)\leq(3/20+2/5000)b+(6/5000)c.
\]
Its exact unrestricted external fibre is
$\sum_j\gauss cj3\,3^{jd_3(K)}$, at most
$\rho_3(c+1)3^{cd_3(K)+c^2/4}$, where
$\rho_3=\prod_{i\geq1}(1-3^{-i})^{-1}$.
Thus the complete cell is
\[
 K_3(n,n-3c)=\frac{\Delta(n,n-3c)}{6^cc!}\rho_3(c+1)
  3^{(3/20+2/5000)cb+(1/4+6/5000)c^2}.
\]
At both parities its Gaussian loss is at least
$(3c-1)b/8+9c^2/16-O(c+1)$ and its coefficient cost is
$O(c\log(n+2))$. Using $\log_2 3<8/5$, the remaining
linear and quadratic coefficients exceed $9/1000$ and $27/1000$,
respectively. Consequently $K_3(n,n-3c)\leq2^{-cn/500}$
uniformly, including $b=0$; summing $c$ gives a geometric row.

In the remaining no-$C_3$ sector, point one actual $A_4$ orbit
and let $J\leq S_b$, $b=n-4$, be its complete complement.
The exact fibre is $1+|\Epi(J,C_3)|+|\Epi(J,A_4)|$.
Call $J$ hot if some index-three normal subgroup $F$ has
$d_2(F)>(1/4+1/200)b$. For fixed $e>0$, the graph injection
$(F,\phi:F\to C_2^r)\mapsto\operatorname{graph}\phi\leq S_{b+2r}$
and the coarse subgroup bound, with $r$ nearest $2eb$, prove
\[
 |\{F\leq S_b:d_2(F)>(1/4+e)b\}|
       \leq2^{b^2/16-e^2b^2+O_e(b^{3/2}+b\log(b+2))}.
\]
Each $F$ has at most $b!$ index-three overgroups, by choosing a
coset generator in $S_b$. The complete hot pointed count, using
the elementary fibre bound $5\cdot2^{(1/2+(\log_2 3)/3)b}$
and its single factor $n!/(24b!)$, is at most
$2^{-n^2/80000}$ after normalization.

For cold $J$ and one surjection $\theta:J\to C_3$, let
$a_\theta$ be the matching $\F_4$ multiplicity in
$H_1(\ker\theta,\F_2)$. The literal cocycle count gives
$4(4^{a_\theta}-1)$ surjections to $A_4$ above $\theta$,
and $2a_\theta\leq(1/4+1/200)b$.
The residual still satisfies the original cutoff, so
$d_3(J)\leq(3/20+2/5000)b+8/5000$.
Its complete cold fibre is at most
\[
 z(b)=5\cdot2^{(1/4+1/200)b+
             (\log_2 3)((3/20+2/5000)b+8/5000)}.
\]
The pointed coefficient is exactly $\Delta(n,n-4)z(n-4)/24$.
Its source exponent is at most $0.49564b$, whereas deleting four
points supplies Gaussian loss $b/2$ and polynomial coefficient
cost. It is therefore at most $2^{-n/500}$ eventually.
Choosing a deterministic actual $A_4$ orbit makes hot and cold
physical inputs disjoint; enlarging each to all pointings pays
each complete positive bound once. This proves the theorem.
\end{proof}
